# Exchange 1

USER: My projector is connected to a JBL Bluetooth speaker. Sometimes the audio becomes choppy when I connect my laptop, and playing video games on the projector causes more eye strain than playing on my monitor. What could cause these problems, and how would you systematically troubleshoot them?

ASSISTANT: Those are two separate problems. For the choppy audio, the likely causes are Bluetooth interference and bandwidth. Bluetooth shares the 2.4 gigahertz band with Wi-Fi, microwaves and other devices, so a busy laptop Wi-Fi connection can crowd it. Distance, obstacles, a weak speaker battery, or the speaker also being connected to another device can all hurt. The laptop may also be using a low-quality codec, or its Bluetooth driver may be old. To troubleshoot, first move the laptop and speaker close with a clear line of sight. Then disconnect the speaker from every other device, and test with the laptop on a 5 gigahertz Wi-Fi band or on ethernet. Update the laptop's Bluetooth and audio drivers, and test the speaker with a phone to see if the fault follows the laptop. If it does, try a wired connection, or a Bluetooth transmitter with a better codec. For the eye strain, think about brightness and contrast in a dark room, lower sharpness and resolution than a monitor, input lag and motion blur, a larger viewing area, and a lack of ambient light. Try lowering projector brightness, adding a soft light behind you, checking the resolution, enabling game mode, and taking breaks.
