# Exchange 1

USER: My projector is connected to a JBL Bluetooth speaker. Sometimes the audio becomes choppy when I connect my laptop, and playing video games on the projector causes more eye strain than playing on my monitor. What could cause these problems, and how would you systematically troubleshoot them?

ASSISTANT: For the choppy audio, the likely causes are radio interference and the projector working harder. Bluetooth shares the 2.4 gigahertz band with Wi-Fi, and when your laptop connects, the projector may be decoding higher-resolution video or surround audio while also sending Bluetooth, so it drops packets. Distance and obstacles between projector and speaker make it worse. To troubleshoot, change one thing at a time. Play the same audio from a different source to see if it's the laptop. Set the laptop's sound output to plain stereo at 48 kilohertz. Move your Wi-Fi to 5 gigahertz and move the speaker closer. If the projector has a headphone or optical output, a cable removes the problem entirely, and also fixes Bluetooth lag in games. For eye strain, common culprits are a picture too bright or too dim for the room, sitting too close so the image fills your vision, keystone correction softening the image, a low refresh rate or motion blur, and on single-chip DLP projectors, the rainbow flicker effect. Try game mode, turn off keystone, keep some soft room light, and sit farther back, comparing after each change.
