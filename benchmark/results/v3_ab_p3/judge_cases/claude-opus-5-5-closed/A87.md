Choppy audio: the speaker may also be paired with the laptop, so the laptop competes for the connection (multipoint); 2.4 GHz interference from Wi-Fi and the laptop's own Bluetooth; distance or obstacles; a weak Bluetooth chip or codec in the projector. Steps, one change at a time: turn off the laptop's Bluetooth or unpair the speaker from it, move the devices closer, put the laptop on 5 GHz Wi-Fi, update the firmware, and test a wired audio link or a low-latency transmitter. # Exchange 1

USER: My projector is connected to a JBL Bluetooth speaker. Sometimes the audio becomes choppy when I connect my laptop, and playing video games on the projector causes more eye strain than playing on my monitor. What could cause these problems, and how would you systematically troubleshoot them?

ASSISTANT: These are probably two separate problems. Choppy Bluetooth audio usually comes from radio interference or a connection tug-of-war. Bluetooth shares the two point four gigahertz band with Wi-Fi, so a laptop streaming nearby can crowd it out, and many JBL speakers pair with two devices at once, so the speaker may be juggling the projector and your laptop. Change one thing at a time: turn off the laptop's Bluetooth or unpair it and see if the stutter stops, move the laptop to five gigahertz Wi-Fi, shorten the distance to the speaker, update firmware, and if possible use a wired audio cable, which also removes the lag Bluetooth adds in games. For eye strain, likely causes are a lower refresh rate or more input lag than your monitor, an image too dim for a bright room or too bright for a dark one, the rainbow flicker of single-chip projectors, softness from keystone correction, or sitting too close to a huge image. Test by enabling game mode, using native resolution and the highest refresh rate, turning off keystone, adjusting room light, and sitting farther back.
