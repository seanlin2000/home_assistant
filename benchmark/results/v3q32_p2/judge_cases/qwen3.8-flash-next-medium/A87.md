Reference sketch (what a correct answer must contain):
Choppy audio: the speaker may also be paired with the laptop, so the laptop competes for the connection (multipoint); 2.4 GHz interference from Wi-Fi and the laptop's own Bluetooth; distance or obstacles; a weak Bluetooth chip or codec in the projector. Steps, one change at a time: turn off the laptop's Bluetooth or unpair the speaker from it, move the devices closer, put the laptop on 5 GHz Wi-Fi, update the firmware, and test a wired audio link or a low-latency transmitter. Eye strain: brightness against room light, an image that fills more of the field of view so the eyes and head move more, sitting too close, soft focus or keystone distortion, motion blur or input lag, and with DLP projectors the rainbow effect from the color wheel; also blinking less during games. Suggestions: a darker room or balanced brightness, a game mode, correct focus and seating distance, and breaks. Should separate the two problems and give an order of tests.

# Exchange 1

USER: My projector is connected to a JBL Bluetooth speaker. Sometimes the audio becomes choppy when I connect my laptop, and playing video games on the projector causes more eye strain than playing on my monitor. What could cause these problems, and how would you systematically troubleshoot them?

ASSISTANT: Choppy audio usually means the Bluetooth link is out of bandwidth or being interfered with, not that the speaker is broken. Bluetooth shares the 2.4 GHz band with Wi‑Fi and leaks from USB 3 ports and hubs, and a projector's Bluetooth receiver plus a laptop dongle competing for the same channel will stutter under game audio.

Troubleshoot by isolation, one change at a time: move the speaker closer with clear line of sight, then disconnect every other Bluetooth device. Test with the laptop's Wi‑Fi switched off, and move Wi‑Fi to 5 GHz. Keep USB 3 hubs away from the projector. Update projector firmware and laptop Bluetooth drivers, switch codec from AAC to SBC, unpair and re‑pair, then test the same speaker with a phone. If the phone is clean, the laptop is the problem.

For eye strain, the suspects are low effective brightness and contrast, low refresh rate or judder from frame‑rate mismatch, slow pixel response causing motion blur, pulse‑width lamp dimming that flickers, glare off the screen, and lower sharpness forcing your eyes to keep refocusing. Raise frame rate to match the projector's refresh, enable its game or low‑latency mode, disable eco or dynamic lamp modes, add a soft bias light behind you, dim the room rather than crank the lamp, and check flicker by filming the screen in slow motion.
