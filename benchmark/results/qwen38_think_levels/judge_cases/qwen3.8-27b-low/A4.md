USER: My plant is growing new leaves quickly near the top, but its older lower leaves are gradually yellowing and dying. The plant is otherwise healthy. What are the most likely explanations, and what information would you want before deciding whether this is normal or a problem?

ASSISTANT: The most likely explanation, and by far the most common one, is that this is just normal leaf cycling. A lot of houseplants — pothos, spider plants, peace lilies, snake plants, many tropicals — grow new leaves at the top and eventually drop the oldest ones at the bottom. The plant is redirecting its resources upward, and the lower leaves simply run their course. If it's one or two leaves a week, the rest of the plant looks vigorous, and the yellowing starts at the leaf tip or the whole leaf at once, that's almost certainly just aging.

That said, there are a few other things worth ruling out. Nitrogen deficiency looks very similar — older leaves yellow first because nitrogen is mobile in the plant and gets shuffled up to new growth. Underwatering can do it too, since the lower leaves are the farthest from the water and the plant sacrifices them first. Overwatering and early root rot can cause the same pattern, though usually the yellowing comes with soft, mushy stems or a foul smell from the soil.

The single most useful thing to know is what species of plant this is. A pothos dropping a lower leaf every couple of weeks is perfectly normal; the same thing happening to a ficus or a snake plant in a shorter timeframe is more worth investigating. Second, I'd want to know how often you water it and whether the soil dries out between waterings. And third, how fast is this happening — one leaf a month versus several a week tells you very different things.

If you can tell me what the plant is, I can give you a much sharper answer.
