# Exchange 1

USER: Someone says that because an MoE model only activates 4 billion parameters per token, a 4 GB GPU should be enough to run it. Is that correct? Explain exactly why or why not.

ASSISTANT: No, that's not correct, and the mistake is a common one. The "4 billion active parameters" figure tells you about compute, not memory.

Here's the key distinction. In a Mixture of Experts model, every token gets routed to a subset of the experts. So for any single token, only a fraction of the total weights actually participate in the calculation. That's what "4 billion active" means: the FLOPs per token are roughly the same as a dense 4-billion model, which makes it fast to generate tokens.

But the memory story is completely different. The GPU has to hold every expert's weights in memory at all times, because you don't get to choose in advance which expert a future token will be routed to. The router makes that decision per token, at runtime. So the memory requirement is set by the total parameter count, not the active count.

In practice, a model with 4 billion active parameters typically has a total somewhere in the range of 15 to 40 billion parameters. Stored in 16-bit precision, that's 30 to 80 gigabytes of weights alone. Even with aggressive 4-bit quantization, you're looking at roughly 8 to 20 gigabytes. Then on top of the weights you still need space for the KV cache as context grows, intermediate activations, and CUDA overhead.

So a 4 gigabyte GPU is short by a factor of several, even in the most optimistic quantized scenario. The "4 billion active" number is a statement about speed per token, not about how much the model weighs in RAM.
