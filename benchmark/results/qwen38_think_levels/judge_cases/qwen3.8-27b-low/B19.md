So it runs, but it's not comfortable.

Now, your second question — whether that "it fits and runs at 17 tokens per second" is enough to predict a good voice-assistant experience — and the honest answer is no, not by itself. Here's why.

For a voice assistant, the metric that matters most is time to first audible response. That's driven primarily by how fast the model processes its prompt, called prefill — your system instructions, conversation history, whatever context is loaded — before it even starts generating the first word. The 17 tokens per second figure is a generation speed, not a prefill speed. A 27B model's prefill on a card with only 288 gigabytes per second of memory bandwidth is going to be noticeably slower than for an 8B model, and I couldn't find a specific prefill benchmark for Qwen3.8-27B on this exact GPU. That's the number you'd actually need to estimate whether the first word comes out in one second or three.

Second, 17 tokens per second is fine for a short assistant reply. A typical voice response is 20 to 40 tokens, which at 17 per second means about one to two and a half seconds of pure generation time. That's within the range people tolerate in conversation, but it's not snappy, and it assumes the model gives you a short answer. A 27B model with thinking mode on by default could easily produce longer, more verbose responses before the TTS engine has anything to speak, pushing perceived latency further out.

Third, the full pipeline includes speech-to-text before the LLM and text-to-speech after it, and those add their own latency that the "17 tokens per second" figure says nothing about.

So what the search actually tells you is that the card is at the very edge of being able to host this model, at a quantization level that will cost some quality. Whether the experience feels good depends on prefill speed, how chatty the model is in a voice role, and the rest of your pipeline — none of which the "it runs at 17 tok/s" answer covers. If you want a concrete prediction, the next thing to look for would be a prefill benchmark for Qwen3.8-27B specifically on a 4060 Ti, or just test it with a realistic system prompt and measure end to end.
