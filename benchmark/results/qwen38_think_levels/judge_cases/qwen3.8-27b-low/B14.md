ASSISTANT: The short version is that the memory sitting on graphics cards became the tight, expensive link in the chain, and the main reason memory got tight is the AI buildout itself. It's not a repeat of the 2021 shortage, which was driven by crypto mining and pandemic logistics; this one has a structural cause.

Here's the chain of events. Modern GPUs use GDDR memory on the board, and GDDR7 in particular is the scarcest class right now. The big memory makers are prioritizing high-bandwidth memory and server DRAM contracts for AI accelerators over the consumer-card memory that graphics cards depend on, and NVIDIA's own CFO said on the record that the memory scarcity is being driven in large part by the AI buildout. TrendForce has been tracking the cost on the flip side: conventional DRAM contract prices jumped something like ninety-three to ninety-eight percent quarter over quarter in the first quarter of this year, and are still forecast to climb another ten to fifteen percent this quarter. Micron is making record profits off of it, so memory makers are clearly the winners here while buyers pay the difference.

The reason this lands so hard on your card specifically is how NVIDIA structures its supply. It sells the GPU and memory to its board partners as a bundled kit, so when the memory half of that kit gets more expensive, the whole kit price goes up, and that cost flows straight to the shelf. MSI, one of the largest partners, told investors it expects a twenty percent supply shortfall from NVIDIA and gaming hardware prices up fifteen to thirty percent this year.

There are secondary factors, but they're smaller. Tariffs and trade policy on chips are a wildcard, though the current 2026 duties reportedly don't actually cover finished graphics cards, so memory and wafer costs explain far more of the increase than tariffs do.

On whether it eases: the forecasts are not optimistic for this year. Because the demand is sustained hyperscaler spending rather than a speculative boom, a quick reversal is unlikely, and new memory capacity mostly arrives from mid-2027 into 2028. So if you're planning a purchase, the realistic picture is that prices stay elevated through the year rather than dropping.
