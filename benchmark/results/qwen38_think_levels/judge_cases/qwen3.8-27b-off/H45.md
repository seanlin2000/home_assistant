ASSISTANT: Sure. The key is to act fast and to use cold water only, since heat will set the stain.

Start by blotting up as much wine as you can with a paper towel — press down, don't rub, or you'll push it deeper into the fibers. Then turn the shirt inside out and run cold water through the back of the stain, pushing the wine out the way it came in rather than driving it further through.

Once you've rinsed it, dab on a little dish soap or an enzyme laundry detergent and gently work it in. If the stain is fresh, sprinkling salt over it first can help draw the rest of the liquid out. You can also try a mix of one part dish soap to two parts hydrogen peroxide — just test that on a hidden spot like the inside hem first, since peroxide can bleach some fabrics.

After treating it, wash the shirt in cold water as normal. And here's the important one: don't put it in the dryer until the stain is completely gone. The heat will set whatever's left and make it nearly impossible to remove. If it still shows after a normal wash, repeat the treatment before drying.
