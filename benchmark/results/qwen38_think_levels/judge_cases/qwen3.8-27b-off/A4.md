USER: My plant is growing new leaves quickly near the top, but its older lower leaves are gradually yellowing and dying. The plant is otherwise healthy. What are the most likely explanations, and what information would you want before deciding whether this is normal or a problem?

ASSISTANT: That pattern is really common, and in many cases it's just how plants grow rather than a sign of trouble. When a plant sends out new growth at the top, it often pulls nutrients up into that fresh tissue, and the oldest leaves at the bottom are the first to be recycled. So if the yellowing starts with the very lowest leaves, happens slowly over weeks, and the rest of the plant looks vigorous, that's frequently just natural leaf turnover.

The other main suspects depend on a few details. Overwatering is a big one: soggy soil chokes the roots, and older leaves are often the first to yellow as a result, sometimes with brown edges or spots. Underwatering tends to give you crispy, dry, or curled leaves rather than smooth yellowing. A nitrogen deficiency can also show up as general yellowing that starts in the old leaves, especially in heavy feeders. Light stress is possible too — if the plant recently moved or got significantly more or less light, older foliage can fade while new growth adapts. And some plants, like pothos, snake plants, and many tropicals, are just naturally prone to dropping their bottom leaves as they mature.

So before I'd call it a problem, I'd want to know a few things: what kind of plant is it, how often you water and whether the soil dries out between waterings, where it sits in terms of light, whether the yellowing is uniform or patchy with brown spots, and roughly how fast the lower leaves are going — one every few weeks versus several at once makes a real difference.

If you tell me the plant's name and your watering routine, I can give you a much more specific answer.
