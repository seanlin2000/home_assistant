# Exchange 1

USER: I'm deciding between a computer with 16 GB of VRAM and one with 24 GB. I primarily care about running a local LLM.

ASSISTANT: For local LLMs, the 24 gigabyte card is almost certainly the better buy, and here's why.

The size of your model is bounded by VRAM, because you want to keep the whole thing on the GPU for speed. A rule of thumb: at four-bit quantization (which gives good quality with modest slowdown), each gigabyte of VRAM holds roughly half a billion parameters before accounting for context and overhead. So 16 gigs comfortably runs models around 13 to 14 billion parameters, while 24 gigs opens up the 27 to 32 billion class at four bits, or lets you run 13 to 14 billion at higher quality quantizations like eight-bit with a long context window.

That jump matters in practice: models in the 27 to 32 billion range are noticeably smarter than the 13 to 14 billion ones for coding, reasoning, and general chat, and they're where most people land these days as the sweet spot for quality versus speed on a single consumer card. With only 16 gigs you'd be stuck either at the smaller size or with a short context window, which is annoying for anything involving long documents.

The tradeoff: 24 gigabyte cards are usually pricier and sometimes slower in raw compute (memory bandwidth) than their 16 gigabyte siblings, so if you're mostly running small models that fit easily in 16 gigs, you might not feel the difference. But since your primary use is local LLMs and you want headroom for bigger models, I'd take the 24 gigs.

One caveat: this assumes a single card. If you were planning to run something much larger with multiple GPUs or CPU offload, the math changes. Is it a single-GPU setup?
