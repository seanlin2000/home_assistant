USER: Explain why VRAM capacity can matter more than raw GPU speed when choosing hardware for a local LLM. Answer in a way that would sound natural if spoken aloud in under 45 seconds.

ASSISTANT: Here's the thing: a local LLM has to sit entirely in your graphics card's memory while it runs. The model's weights, plus the working memory it needs to track the conversation, all have to fit in VRAM at once. If they don't, the system starts spilling over to your regular memory or disk, and things crawl from blazing fast to nearly unusable.

So you can have a GPU that's technically quicker but only has, say, twelve gigabytes, and it can't run a big model at all. Meanwhile, a slower card with thirty-two gigabytes can load the same model and actually answer your question. Capacity decides whether the model runs; raw speed only decides how fast it runs once it fits. That's why people buy more memory than they think they need.
