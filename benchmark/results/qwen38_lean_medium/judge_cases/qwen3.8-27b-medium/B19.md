ASSISTANT: Short answer on the hardware: sort of. "Qwen3.8" is a family released in the summer of 2026, and the locally runnable one people are asking about is the 27B dense model. On a 4060 Ti with 16 gigabytes, the standard 4-bit build needs about 18.3 gigabytes at an 8,000-token context, so it does not fully fit. You'd have to drop to a 3-bit quant to fit the whole thing in VRAM, or run the 4-bit with a few gigabytes spilling into system RAM. One calculator site estimates that offloaded configuration at roughly 7 tokens per second, and a 3-bit build that fits fully in VRAM would not be dramatically faster. So: yes, it can technically run, but slowly and at reduced quantization.

Now the more interesting part of your question: no, that answer is not enough to predict a good voice-assistant experience, for a few reasons.

First, "it fits in memory" and "it feels good as a voice assistant" are different questions. The thing that matters for voice is latency — how long from the moment you stop talking until you start hearing an answer. That depends on first-token time, which depends heavily on context length and prompt size, not just decode speed. A 7 tokens-per-second card might produce a thirty-word answer in about four seconds, which is borderline for conversation, but the sources I found give estimates, not measurements, and the spread on the offloaded number was plus or minus 20 percent.

Second, quantization quality matters more than people expect for an assistant. A 3-bit Qwen3.8 is a meaningfully dumber model than the 4-bit one, and a voice assistant is a demanding use case — you want reliable instruction-following, short answers, and no hallucinated confidence, and aggressive quantization is exactly where models get flakier about that. The "does it fit" tables treat all quantizations that fit as equally good, which is not true.

Third, the LLM is only one link in a voice-assistant chain. You also need speech-to-text, the model, and text-to-speech, each adding its own latency, plus how well this particular model handles short conversational prompts versus long-form tasks. Qwen3.8 was pitched around coding and long-horizon agentic work, so I have no evidence from these sources that it's tuned for rapid back-and-forth at all.

If you're serious about this, the useful experiment is not a VRAM calculator but a timing test: run the 3-bit or offloaded 4-bit build locally, ask it a few short assistant-style questions, and time from end-of-utterance to start-of-speech. If that comes in around two seconds or less, it'll feel fine; if it's four or five, it'll feel like waiting on a search engine. I can also tell you the 4060 Ti runs smaller models — the 8 to 14 gigabyte ones — at 20 to 30 tokens per second fully in VRAM, and for a voice assistant a fast, smaller model is usually the better trade than a slow 27B.
