ASSISTANT: Yes, it can, but not out of the box — it takes a custom firmware build.

Here's how it works. The Voice PE runs wake word detection entirely on the device, on the ESP32-S3 chip, using a technology called microWakeWord. In the stock firmware, you can only pick from three built-in options: Okay Nabu, Hey Jarvis, and Hey Mycroft. That's confirmed by Nabu Casa's official support article, which is the source I'd trust most for what the device supports as-is, since Nabu Casa is the company that makes the hardware and the firmware.

To use your own wake word, you have to train a custom microWakeWord model, then take control of the device in ESPHome, put your model file in the firmware, and recompile and flash it yourself. The Home Assistant community forum backs this up — in a thread where someone was struggling to get a custom wake word working, the accepted answer was exactly that: the device uses on-device microWakeWord, not the openWakeWord server integration you might be familiar with, so you need to train a microWakeWord model and flash a custom firmware build. There's even an active community project, "open-voice-pe," that has maintained custom firmware for the Voice PE for over a year, with a December 2025 version that adds custom microWakeWord models alongside the stock ones.

The trade-off worth knowing: once you take over the firmware, you lose the "set it and forget it" nature of the official setup — you have to reapply your changes whenever a new official firmware comes out, per the community thread. So the short version is: officially, no; practically, yes, if you're comfortable flashing custom ESPHome firmware.
