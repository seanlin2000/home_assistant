USER: My plant is growing new leaves quickly near the top, but its older lower leaves are gradually yellowing and dying. The plant is otherwise healthy. What are the most likely explanations, and what information would you want before deciding whether this is normal or a problem?

ASSISTANT: If a plant is pushing new growth at the top while the oldest leaves yellow and drop, that's often just normal cycling. Plants recycle nutrients from their oldest leaves when the roots can't feed everything, especially in active growth. But there are a few other likely culprits worth ruling out.

Overwatering is the most common one. When roots sit in soggy soil they suffocate, the plant can't take up nutrients, and the lower leaves — closest to where the damage starts — yellow first. The opposite, underwatering or a nutrient deficiency like nitrogen, looks similar, though with underwatering the leaves usually feel dry and crispy rather than soft.

A few things I'd want to know before calling it normal or a problem:

First, what kind of plant is it? This pattern is completely normal for things like pothos and snake plants, which drop lower leaves as they climb, while the same thing on a fiddle-leaf fig or an avocado would worry me more.

Second, how are you watering? If the soil stays wet for days between waterings, that's the first thing I'd fix. You can check by sticking a finger in about two inches down or lifting the pot — if it feels heavy and smells musty, it's too wet.

Third, how fast is the yellowing happening, and what do the leaves look like as they go? A few leaves per month that fade slowly is usually fine. Several leaves in a couple of weeks, or yellowing with dark spots, brown tips, or a soft mushy base, points to a real problem.

And one practical test: look at the pot for drainage holes and check that it has any. If it doesn't, that's often the whole story on its own.

Tell me the plant and roughly how you're watering it, and I can give you a much more confident read.
