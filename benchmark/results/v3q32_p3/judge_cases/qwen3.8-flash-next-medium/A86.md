Reference sketch (what a correct answer must contain):
The Hue Bridge is the local hub: it talks to the bulbs over Zigbee and keeps scenes and automations. Share control by inviting the other people in the Hue app or letting them use it on the home network, add physical Hue switches for anyone without a phone, and connect a voice assistant (Apple Home through the bridge, Alexa, Google Home, or a local controller such as Home Assistant). If the internet goes down: the bridge, its automations, the Hue app on the local network and physical switches keep working; Apple Home with a home hub keeps working locally; cloud voice assistants such as Alexa and Google stop taking commands, and control from outside the home stops. A local voice setup avoids that dependency.

## How to restore internet access to the Hue Bridge
### Give the Bridge a fresh DHCP address
A stuck DHCP lease can cause “no internet” even when local control works. In your router admin page, find the DHCP leases list and locate the Hue Bridge entry by its IP or MAC address. Remove the current lease (or “release/clear” it if your router offers that), then restart the Bridge power by unplugging it for 30 seconds. After the Bridge reconnects, it should request a new DHCP lease and receive a valid IP and gateway path. This often resolves DNS/gateway routing issues tied to an old address.
## Bluetooth, app access, and non-Bridge setups

Some other smart lighting systems advertise “local control” or “works without internet” in ways that sound similar to Hue—but the setup and failure points differ.
