ASSISTANT: When running a Local Large Language Model on your computer, VRAM capacity often matters more than raw processing speed because the model itself can be so large that it won't fit in your video memory at all. If you try to run a model that exceeds your VRAM, the system will crash or resort to using your much slower RAM and CPU, making inference painfully slow. A powerful GPU with tiny VRAM might be faster per calculation than a weaker one, but if it can't hold the entire model in its memory, you're left with nothing to work with. So for local LLMs, fitting the model into VRAM is the absolute first requirement before speed becomes relevant.
