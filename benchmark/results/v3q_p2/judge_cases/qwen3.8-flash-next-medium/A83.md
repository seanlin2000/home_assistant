ASSISTANT: Your twenty thousand dollars buys 0.0333 percent of the company at a sixty million post-money valuation, which works out to roughly one share in every three thousand.

Fifty percent cumulative dilution cuts that stake in half, down to about 0.0167 percent. Applied to a billion dollar sale, that stake is worth around $166,667. So your money grows about 8.3 times over, for a profit of roughly $146,667 before taxes.

Two caveats worth keeping in mind. First, this assumes simple proportional dilution, meaning your percentage is just halved and nothing else changes. In practice, new investors with liquidation preferences get paid out of that billion dollars before common shareholders see anything, and a fully diluted share count can shrink your slice further, so the real number is often lower. Second, the exit has to be structured as a sale of the whole company; if it is a stock-for-stock acquisition, the payout is not cash in hand.
