The choppy audio is almost certainly a radio problem. Bluetooth lives in the 2.4 gigahertz band, and so does most Wi-Fi, so when your laptop is pulling real traffic over 2.4 gigahertz while simultaneously streaming audio to the JBL, the two are sharing one crowded lane. Bluetooth tries to dodge Wi-Fi with adaptive frequency hopping, jumping among its channels hundreds of times a second, but when a Wi-Fi channel is wide or the band is congested, it runs out of clean slots and packets get dropped, which is exactly the stutter you hear. This is worse on laptops because the Wi-Fi and Bluetooth radios are usually on the same little chip and share an antenna, so they have to negotiate for airtime internally. That is why the symptom appears "when I connect my laptop" but not when something else is the source.

The other candidates worth knowing: unshielded USB 3.0 devices radiate broadband noise right into the 2.4 to 2.5 gigahertz range, so a plugged-in external drive, dock, or HDMI-to-USB adapter can act like a local jammer. Windows power management can briefly power down the Bluetooth adapter to save energy. And if the audio codec is a high-bitrate one, or the laptop is under heavy CPU load while gaming, the audio buffer can underrun and sound choppy even with a clean radio link.

Here is how I would narrow it down, one variable at a time. First, switch the laptop to a 5 or 6 gigahertz Wi-Fi network and test the same local audio.
