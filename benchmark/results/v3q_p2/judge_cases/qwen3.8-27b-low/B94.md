## First or second class?
Second class is comfortable, clean and how most Swiss travel — it is the right default. First class buys you quieter cars, wider seats and better odds of a window seat on busy panoramic routes in July–August. ## Is the Train Route From Interlaken to Zermatt Scenic?
Not entirely. The train route from Zermatt to Interlaken via Spiez and Visp is not entirely scenic. In fact, between Spiez and Visp, the train goes through the Lötschberg Tunnel.

Day one, land in Switzerland and take the train into Lucerne. There's no seat reservation needed or even possible on this one, so you just board. Settle into Interlaken that evening, which sits right between Lake Thun and Lake Brienz.

Day three, stay in the Interlaken region for a viewpoint without the effort. The two lakes are your playground — hop on a steamship on Lake Thun, again covered by the pass. For a mountain view without strenuous hiking, the Harder Kulm funicular up above Interlaken gives you a flat platform with a panorama over both lakes and the Jungfrau range. If you're feeling brave for one short walk, the Lauterbrunnen valley is the most famous, but the classic hikes there (to Trümmelbach or Mürren) are long and uphill, so I'd skip them and just take the valley's scenery from the train and a cable car to Grindelwald or Mürren, both flat at the top.

It's roughly two hours. No reservation required, pass covers it.
