Reference sketch (what a correct answer must contain):
The Hue Bridge is the local hub: it talks to the bulbs over Zigbee and keeps scenes and automations. Share control by inviting the other people in the Hue app or letting them use it on the home network, add physical Hue switches for anyone without a phone, and connect a voice assistant (Apple Home through the bridge, Alexa, Google Home, or a local controller such as Home Assistant). If the internet goes down: the bridge, its automations, the Hue app on the local network and physical switches keep working; Apple Home with a home hub keeps working locally; cloud voice assistants such as Alexa and Google stop taking commands, and control from outside the home stops. A local voice setup avoids that dependency.

### Wi‑Fi and Zigbee channel conflict
Both Zigbee and 2.4 GHz Wi‑Fi share the same frequency band. If your router uses a channel that overlaps with Zigbee’s default channel (15 or 25 on Hue), you’ll see intermittent dropouts. Check your router’s 2.4 GHz channel – avoid 1, 6, and 11 if possible. The Hue Bridge automatically picks a Zigbee channel; you can change it in the app under Settings → Bridge → Zigbee channel (try 11, 15, 20, or 25). ASSISTANT: Here's the setup I'd actually recommend for your situation.

The core is a single Hue Bridge — or the newer Bridge Pro if you can get it, since it adds Matter and improved local control. The Bridge sits on your network over Ethernet and runs a private Zigbee mesh for all the bulbs, so the lights themselves never depend on your internet. Pick one person to be the "owner" of the Hue home, and they add all the lights, rooms, and scenes in the Hue app.

For the other people in the apartment, you don't share a login. The Hue app has member management — in the owner's app it's under Settings, then Account, then Your Home (some versions label it Home access). From there the owner invites each person by email, and each of them accepts on their own phone with their own Hue account. You assign each person "Lighting" rights for everyday control, or "Administrator" if they should be able to change settings and manage members too. Everyone then sees the same rooms, scenes, and devices and can just tap to change things, and you can revoke anyone's access at any time.

For voice, connect that same Hue home to whichever assistant you use — Alexa, Google Home, or Apple Home — through each assistant's app. Everyone in the apartment can then speak to their own device or their phone, and it all points at the same shared lights. The clean thing about this architecture is that app control and voice control both ride on the one local Bridge, so they don't fight each other.

Now the outage question, which is where it gets a little nuanced. The Zigbee mesh and the Bridge are local, so when the internet drops, in-home control via the Hue app on the same Wi-Fi keeps working, and any physical Hue switches you have — the Tap, dimmers, wall switches — keep working too, because they talk straight to the Bridge. What breaks is anything that has to reach the cloud: remote control from outside the house, and the usual cloud-based voice assistants. In the standard Alexa or Google mode, "turn on the light" goes to the cloud, so with the internet down those voice commands stop.

The one improving exception is local Matter control. As of a recent Google Home update this past fall, the Google app can control Hue lights locally over the network during an outage, and on supported Echo devices, local Matter voice can keep basic commands working without the internet. So if you're pairing with a newer Echo or Google Home device and Matter is set up, simple "on/off the living room light" commands have a decent chance of surviving an internet outage — but I'd treat that as the newer, device-dependent case rather than a guarantee, since it depends on which assistant hardware you actually have and whether it supports local voice. If bulletproof offline voice matters a lot to you, the heavier-weight answer is a local home automation hub like Home Assistant, which can drive Hue fully offline and run its own local voice assistant, but that's a bigger project than most apartments need.

So short version: one Bridge as the local hub, one owner account with everyone else invited as members, and your assistant of choice hooked to the same home. You lose cloud voice and away-from-home access when the internet dies, but the lights and local control keep going.
