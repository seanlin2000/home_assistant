The deep causes were economic and structural, and they predate all three events. The Soviet command economy was simply running out of steam — it couldn't match Western productivity, oil prices collapsed in the mid-1980s which gutted state revenue, and maintaining superpower status through arms races and prop up satellite states had become unaffordable. That was the underlying gravity pulling everything down.

Glasnost — openness — let citizens finally see how bad things were, and perestroika tried to restructure the economy without dismantling one-party rule. The reform was meant to save the system, but it backfired: once people could criticize the government, they did, and once national republics saw Moscow weakening, they started asking for real independence.
