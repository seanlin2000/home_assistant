## Why does each big bank pay what it pays?
### Chase
Chase is the largest US bank by assets, and that scale is exactly why its standard savings account pays just 0.01% APY — deposits flow in regardless of rate. The Premier Savings tier nudges the rate up only fractionally, and only if you also maintain a linked Chase Premier Plus or Premier Platinum Checking account. The structure is built to keep deposits, not to grow them.
See full Chase comparison →
### Bank of America
Bank of America's Preferred Rewards program ties account perks to investment balances held at Merrill — not to your savings balance alone. Those perks accrue to credit cards and investments rather than the savings rate, so Advantage Savings stays put no matter how much you consolidate at Merrill. The standard rate sits at 0.04% APY, and even Diamond-tier members earn rates that fall far short of any top HYSA.
See full Bank of America comparison →
### Wells Fargo
Wells Fargo's standard Way2Save savings pays just 0.01% APY on a $10,000 balance. Its relationship-tier Platinum Savings reaches only 0.05% APY, and only with a linked Wells Fargo Premier Checking account holding the rate in place. Step-ups across balance tiers are negligible. The product structure is engineered so almost no retail customer ever clears a competitive rate.
See full Wells Fargo comparison →
### U.S. Bank
U.S. Bank Smartly® Savings advertises 2.00% APY on balances of $5,000–$24,999.99, but only customers who also keep a linked U.S. Bank Smartly Checking account earn that rate. Without the linked checking, the same savings account drops to 0.05% APY — near the floor that Chase and Bank of America sit at. The two-tier structure is engineered to retain U.S. Bank's primary-banking relationship rather than to compete on standalone yield. For comparison, Leader Bank pays 5.00% APY, a promotional rate, and requires a linked checking account, direct deposit of $1,000+/mo, a minimum opening deposit, and other conditions.
See full U.S. Bank comparison →
### Truist
Truist is the one bank here whose second savings account costs more and pays exactly the same. Truist One Savings, the flagship, pays 0.01% APY at every balance tier in its published disclosure and adds a $5 monthly fee with four waiver paths. Six years after the BB&T–SunTrust merger, the deposit book is stable enough that neither product needs a competitive rate.

### TD Bank

The convenience is real; it is paid for out of the interest.
