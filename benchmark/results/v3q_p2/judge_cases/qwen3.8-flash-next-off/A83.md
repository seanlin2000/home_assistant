Two things worth flagging. First, "50% cumulative dilution" is usually read as your ownership being cut in half, which is what I calculated; if instead it means the company issued new shares equal to 50 percent of the total post-round count, your stake would land closer to 0.0222 percent and about $222,000 at exit. Second, this ignores liquidation preferences. If preferred investors hold a 1x preference stack, some or all of the billion gets paid out to them before common holders see anything, and your number could be materially lower — potentially zero if the preferences cover most of the proceeds. Also assumed: no secondary sales, no option pool effects beyond the stated dilution, and no taxes, which on a $146,000 gain would take a meaningful bite depending on holding period and structure.
