The Hue Bridge is the local hub: it talks to the bulbs over Zigbee and keeps scenes and automations. Share control by inviting the other people in the Hue app or letting them use it on the home network, add physical Hue switches for anyone without a phone, and connect a voice assistant (Apple Home through the bridge, Alexa, Google Home, or a local controller such as Home Assistant). If the internet goes down: the bridge, its automations, the Hue app on the local network and physical switches keep working; Apple Home with a home hub keeps working locally; cloud voice assistants such as Alexa and Google stop taking commands, and control from outside the home stops. A local voice setup avoids that dependency.

## Why local Matter control is a big deal
Google's own Matter support page lays out the difference clearly. Matter is also multi-admin by design. Google's documentation notes that Matter devices can be controlled simultaneously in several certified apps, such as Google Home, Apple Home and Amazon Alexa. With cloud control, your phone or speaker sends a command to the manufacturer's server over the internet, and the server sends it back down to the device. It's simple for companies to build and makes remote access easy. The catch: if your internet connection, the company's servers or the company itself goes away, the device can stop responding to the app.
With local control, the command goes from your phone, a wall switch or an automation straight to a hub or the device over your home network. The internet is only needed for extras like remote access. Local control is also usually faster, because the signal doesn't make a round trip to a data centre.
