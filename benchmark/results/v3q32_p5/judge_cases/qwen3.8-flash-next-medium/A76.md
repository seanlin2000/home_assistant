ASSISTANT: The Cold War ended not with a battle but with the Soviet system exhausting itself. The deep causes were economic: decades of stagnation, a bloated military and arms race that starved consumer production, and the loss of cheap Eastern European markets. Gorbachev's reforms, glasnost and perestroika, were meant to save the system but instead loosened censorship and empowered nationalist movements. The immediate triggers were sharper: the fall of the Berlin Wall in November 1989, which ended Soviet control of Eastern Europe; the failed hardliner coup in August 1991, which destroyed central authority; and Gorbachev's resignation on December 25, 1991.
