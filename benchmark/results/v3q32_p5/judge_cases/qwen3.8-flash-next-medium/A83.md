ASSISTANT: You start with 20,000 divided by 60 million, which is about 0.033 percent of the company, or roughly one part in 3,000.

Fifty percent cumulative dilution cuts that in half, leaving you about 0.017 percent of the fully diluted company.

At a 1 billion dollar sale, your share is about 166,667 dollars.

That's a multiple of 8.33 times your money back, for a profit of roughly 146,667 dollars before taxes.

Two caveats worth flagging. First, this assumes your 50 percent dilution is measured against your original stake and that you have no anti-dilution protection or additional investment. Second, it ignores liquidation preferences, which in practice often sit ahead of common shareholders and can reduce or eliminate this payout.
