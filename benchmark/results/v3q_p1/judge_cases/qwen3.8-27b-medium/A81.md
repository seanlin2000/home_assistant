# Exchange 1

USER: If I run a language model locally on my computer but use an external web-search API, what information can the search provider see? Explain what stays local, what gets transmitted, and how I can minimize data exposure.

ASSISTANT: Good question, and the short answer is: less than you might think, but the one thing that leaves your machine is more revealing than it looks.

What stays local. The model weights, all inference, your full conversation history, the files the model reads from disk, and whatever it generates or writes back. None of that touches the search provider. They have no window into your prompt, your system instructions, or the answers your model produces.

What gets transmitted. The search API only ever receives the query string, plus the normal plumbing of any API call: your IP address, timestamps, a user-agent or client identifier, TLS-level details like which domain you're connecting to, and your API key, which ties every request to your account. Depending on the API, you can optionally send extra fields like context, instructions, or domain filters, and anything you put in those goes to them too. That's it, but there are two subtleties worth knowing.

The first subtlety is the important one: the query is not your words, it's the model's paraphrase of your intent. If you're discussing your medical history, a work project, or a private document, the local model may fold those details into the search query without you noticing, because it's doing its job well. So even though the full prompt stays local, sensitive content can leak in fragments through the query alone.

The second is what the provider does with what it sees. Every search provider retains query logs to some degree, and their privacy policies vary a lot: how long they keep logs, whether they use them for analytics, whether they train on them, whether they sell or share them. That policy is the real variable, so it's worth reading the specific one you use rather than assuming.

On minimizing exposure. Keep the queries thin: instruct the model to search with minimal, generic terms and to never include names, paths, or personal details in queries. Put a thin middleware layer between your model and the API that intercepts and, if you want, redacts or logs outgoing queries, so nothing leaves without passing a check. Don't use the context or instructions fields unless you've reviewed what they contain. Check the provider's retention and data-use policy and pick one that offers minimal logging or a no-retention option. Consider routing requests through a relay server so your home IP isn't permanently tied to the account, and use a scoped API key rather than your main one.

And if you want zero external exposure, the alternative is a self-hosted search layer, like a SearXNG instance, which keeps the queries on your own infrastructure, though you trade that for maintenance work and rate-limit headaches.
