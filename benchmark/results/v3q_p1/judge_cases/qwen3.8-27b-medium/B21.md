## How to read it
The FOMC statement is compared word-for-word against the previous statement by every major financial institution within seconds of release. Key phrase changes signal shifts in the Committee's thinking. Watch for: changes to the inflation assessment ("elevated" → "somewhat elevated" → "returning to"), changes to the labour market language ("strong" → "solid" → "cooling"), and any changes to the guidance phrase (e.g., "some additional policy firming" vs "holding the policy rate steady"). A redlined comparison appears on the Fed's website within minutes of the statement.
Chair Powell's press conference typically contains more information than the statement itself, because journalists ask questions that force clarification. The most market-sensitive moments are usually the answers to: (1) the first question about the rate decision rationale; (2) questions about the conditions for the next cut or hike; (3) questions about inflation progress and the timeline to reaching target. The transcript is posted on the Fed website within 1 hour. Key phrases like "meeting-by-meeting", "data-dependent", and references to specific inflation measures (core PCE, services ex-housing) reveal the FOMC's reaction function.
The Summary of Economic Projections (SEP), which includes the famous "dot plot" of each FOMC participant's rate forecasts, is published four times per year (March, June, September, December meetings). The November meeting does not produce a dot plot. The September 2026 dot plot showed a median forecast of the funds rate at 4.25–4.50% by end-2026 — implying two more 25bp cuts after September. Whether the November cut aligns with or deviates from that path is an important signal about December.
