USER: My projector is connected to a JBL Bluetooth speaker. Sometimes the audio becomes choppy when I connect my laptop, and playing video games on the projector causes more eye strain than playing on my monitor. What could cause these problems, and how would you systematically troubleshoot them?

ASSISTANT: Let me take these one at a time, because they are likely two completely different problems.

First, the choppy audio when you connect your laptop. The most common culprit here is that you have two Bluetooth sources fighting for the same speaker. Your projector and your laptop both want to send audio over Bluetooth, and if both are connected simultaneously, or if the connection is switching back and forth between them, you will get exactly that kind of intermittent stuttering. Bluetooth is also very sensitive to interference from 2.4 gigahertz Wi-Fi, USB 3.0 devices, and other wireless gear, so a crowded frequency environment can degrade the audio stream even when nothing else has changed.

A systematic way to work through it: start by disconnecting your laptop entirely and see if the projector audio is clean on its own. If it is, the problem is confirmed to be related to having two sources connected. Then try this in order. First, make sure only one device is actively sending audio at a time; on your laptop, you can set the JBL speaker as the default output and mute or disconnect any other Bluetooth audio source. Second, move the speaker closer to both devices and remove obstacles, since Bluetooth range drops quickly through walls and furniture. Third, check whether your Wi-Fi router is on 2.4 gigahertz; if it is, switching it to 5 gigahertz can reduce a lot of interference. Fourth, make sure all three devices have their firmware updated, because manufacturers often fix connection bugs in updates. Fifth, try pairing the speaker fresh by forgetting it on every device and re-pairing from scratch, which clears out corrupted pairing profiles. And finally, if you are using a USB 3.0 hub or external drive near the speaker, unplug it temporarily; those can be surprisingly bad interference sources for Bluetooth.

Now, the eye strain when gaming on the projector versus your monitor. This is almost certainly not about the technology itself but about how the projected image is physically different from what you see on a monitor. A few things stand out. Projected images are typically viewed at a much greater distance, which means fine text and small UI elements like health bars or minimap icons become harder to resolve, forcing your eyes to work harder. The ambient light in the room also matters a lot; if there is any light hitting the wall or screen, it reduces contrast, and low contrast is one of the biggest drivers of eye fatigue. Finally, motion handling differs; many projectors have lower refresh rates or more input lag than a gaming monitor, which can create subtle visual discomfort over long sessions.
