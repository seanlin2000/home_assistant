## The Wyoming Voice Architecture: Pipeline Latency Breakdown
To achieve sub-500ms voice execution, the Home Assistant voice pipeline splits processing across edge micro-satellites and a local server running faster-whisper and piper:
- Wake Word (microWakeWord on ESP32-S3): Running a lightweight quantized TensorFlow Lite model directly on the ESP32-S3’s vector instructions. When you say “Okay Nabu”, wake-word detection completes in under40ms locally on the microchip.
- Audio Transport (Wyoming Protocol): The satellite streams 16kHz 16-bit mono PCM audio over TCP/UDP to the Home Assistant server. Transport latency:15ms .
- Speech-to-Text (faster-whisper on GPU/NPU): Utilizing a local NVIDIA RTX 3060, Intel N100, or Coral TPU running`faster-whisper-tiny.en` with INT8 quantization, transcription takes approximately180ms .
- Intent Matching (Home Assistant Assist): The intent engine parses structured YAML intents (“turn off kitchen pendant lights”) in under25ms .
- Text-to-Speech (Piper TTS): Piper synthesizes a warm, human audio confirmation (“Kitchen lights turned off”) in120ms and streams it back to the satellite’s MAX98357A amplifier.

## Wake words
The best open option in 2026 is still openWakeWord. It matches the acoustic shape of a phrase, which is less brittle than matching phoneme patterns.
