## FAQs
### How do high-yield savings accounts work?
High-yield savings accounts offer significantly higher interest rates than traditional savings products, usually 10 times or more. HYSAs are also generally offered by online banks without physical branches, and they may require more steps to withdraw or deposit cash.
### How often do interest rates change?
Interest rates on high-yield savings accounts are variable and can fluctuate daily. In general, though, they change every few months after the Fed meets to adjust the federal funds rate. When the fed funds rate increases, HYSA rates usually follow suit, and vice versa.

Your bank pays interest on your balance, usually compounded daily and credited monthly. The annual percentage yield (APY) already includes compounding, so it’s the number to compare between banks. Unlike a certificate of deposit, the rate is variable: the bank can raise or lower it whenever it chooses, and it usually moves in the same direction as the Federal Reserve’s benchmark rate.
Federal rules no longer cap savings withdrawals at six per month, but many banks still set their own limit or charge a fee for excess transfers. If you plan to move money often, check this before you open.
### 1. Compare APY, but read the conditions
Some headline rates apply only to new customers, only up to a balance cap, or only if you make a monthly deposit. A slightly lower rate with no strings attached may pay more over a full year.
### 2. Confirm deposit insurance
Choose a bank insured by the FDIC or a credit union insured by the NCUA. Some fintech apps aren’t banks themselves; they hold your money at a partner bank. Make sure you know which bank holds your funds and that your total across accounts stays within insurance limits. Our guide to online banks vs. traditional banks covers this in more detail.
### 3. Check fees and minimums
The best accounts charge no monthly maintenance fee and require little or no opening deposit. Avoid accounts that charge a fee if your balance dips below a minimum.
### 4. Make sure transfers are easy
Link the savings account to your everyday checking account. Standard transfers usually take one to three business days, so keep a small cash cushion in checking for immediate needs.
### 5. Look at the bank’s track record
A bank that keeps its rate competitive for years is often better than one with a short teaser rate. Read the fine print on any “promotional” or “boosted” APY.
