ASSISTANT: Your twenty thousand dollars buys about 0.033 percent of the company, since 20,000 divided by a 60 million post-money valuation is roughly one part in three thousand.

After 50 percent cumulative dilution, that slice is cut in half to about 0.0167 percent. At a one billion dollar exit, that stake is worth about $166,700.

So the return multiple is about 8.3 times your money, and the profit, before taxes, is roughly $146,700.

Two things worth flagging. This assumes the dilution is spread across all shareholders proportionally, so your stake is simply halved; if you held pro-rata rights, preferred shares with anti-dilution protection, or you did not participate in later rounds, the number would move. It also assumes the sale price is all cash to shareholders with nothing taken up by liquidation preferences, which in venture deals often gets paid out to later investors before common holders see anything.
