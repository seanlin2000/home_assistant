ASSISTANT: The Soviet Union dissolved on December 25, 1991, but it rotted for years before that. The underlying causes were long-term: a stagnant command economy that couldn't keep pace with the West, the crushing cost of the arms race, and rising nationalism inside its many republics. Three events turned that decay into collapse. First, Gorbachev's perestroika and glasnost in the mid-eighties cracked open the system and let old grievances surface. Second, the fall of the Berlin Wall in late 1989 showed the Eastern Bloc could be abandoned without force. Third, and most immediate, the failed coup against Gorbachev in August 1991 destroyed the last of central authority — after that, republics simply seceded.
