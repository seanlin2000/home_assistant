ASSISTANT: You'd own about 0.033 percent of the company going in — 20,000 divided by 60 million, roughly 1 part in 3,000. Fifty percent cumulative dilution cuts that to about 0.0167 percent. Applied to a 1 billion dollar sale, that's about 166,667 dollars before taxes.

That's a multiple of about 8.3 times your money, so your profit is roughly 146,667 dollars. Two caveats worth flagging: the 50 percent dilution assumption is doing a lot of work here, since how much later rounds dilute you depends on their valuations and whether you have anti-dilution protection or pro rata rights to invest further. And in a real deal, preference stacks from later investors could take a chunk of the 1 billion off the top before common shares like yours see anything.
