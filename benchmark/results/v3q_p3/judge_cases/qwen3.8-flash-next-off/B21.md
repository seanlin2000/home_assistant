ASSISTANT: I need to correct the premise before anything else, because it changes the whole answer.

The Fed did not cut rates to zero last month. At its October 28 meeting, the FOMC held the federal funds target range at 3.75% to 4%, and it actually raised rates a quarter point in September — two governors dissented because they wanted another hike, not a cut. Rates have been roughly where they are now for about a year.

Meanwhile, mortgage rates are nowhere near zero either. As of this week, the national average on a 30-year fixed is somewhere between about 7% and 7.2%, depending on which survey you trust (Bankrate has 7.22%, Forbes' research center around 7.0–7.1%), with 15-year loans in the low 6s. New York's average is right around 7.1%.

So refinancing right now only makes sense if your current rate is meaningfully above that — for example, if you're sitting on a 7.5% to 8%+ mortgage from the last couple of years, or you're carrying something with a variable rate or a higher margin. If you locked in during the 2020–2021 era at 3%, refinancing today would almost certainly be a bad move no matter what the Fed does next.

To give you a real answer rather than a general one, I'd need three numbers: your current rate, roughly how much you still owe, and how many years are left on the loan. If you tell me those, I can calculate the monthly savings, the total interest you'd save, and how long it would take to recoup closing costs — typically around 2% of the loan amount.
