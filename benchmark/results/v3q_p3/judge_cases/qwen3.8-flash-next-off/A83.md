ASSISTANT: Your twenty thousand dollars buys about 0.0333 percent of the company at a sixty million post-money valuation — roughly one share in three thousand. After fifty percent cumulative dilution, that stake is cut to about 0.0167 percent, and applied to a billion dollar exit it comes out to approximately $166,667 before taxes.

So your profit is about $146,667, and your return multiple is 8.33 times your money — the same as saying the company grew from sixty million to a billion, an 16.7-times increase, but you only captured half of it because of the dilution.

Two caveats worth keeping in mind. First, this assumes straight pro-rata treatment with no liquidation preferences: if preferred investors hold a 1x or higher preference, common holders like you could receive materially less than your proportional slice. Second, fifty percent cumulative dilution is a rough average; the actual outcome depends on how much was issued in each round and at what prices, and whether you participated in later rounds to maintain your percentage.
