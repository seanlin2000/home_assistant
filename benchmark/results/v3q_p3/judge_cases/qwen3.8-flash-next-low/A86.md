Also make sure your home's location is set in the Hue app settings, or sunrise and sunset triggers can't be computed.

**Add physical controls.** For an apartment shared with people who won't all keep the app handy, Hue Dimmer Switches, Smart Buttons or a Tap Dial paired to the bridge are the most robust part of the whole system. They talk over Zigbee directly, need no phone, no login and no internet, and they also strengthen the mesh since every mains-powered light repeats the signal.

**If the internet goes down.** Two different failures, and it's worth separating them.

When only your internet is out, but the router and bridge are still up: the lights themselves are fine, Zigbee is a separate network from your Wi‑Fi, and control on your home network generally continues. Physical Hue switches, buttons and motion sensors keep working. Schedules and scenes stored on the bridge keep firing. The Hue app on a phone connected to your Wi‑Fi usually still works, because it talks to the bridge through your router rather than through Philips' servers. What you do lose is voice commands, since Alexa and Google route through their clouds, and out-of-home control from the office or while traveling. Arrival and departure automations also stop, since geofencing needs the internet and a powered phone. One subtler thing: the bridge syncs its clock from the internet, so after several days offline a schedule can trigger slightly off-time, and a brief reconnect resyncs it. I'd give you a caveat here — Philips does not publish a feature-by-feature guarantee for what survives an internet-only outage, and app behavior depends on your configuration, so treat the app path as "usually fine" and physical switches as the thing you actually count on.

When your local network is down, which is worse: the bridge is unreachable by phones and by voice assistants, because it plugs into the router for exactly that path. Switch off the router and stop expecting app control, even though the lights are still alive. Hue switches paired to the bridge still work over Zigbee. There's one exception: the Hue Bridge Pro connects by Wi‑Fi and doesn't need an Ethernet cable.
