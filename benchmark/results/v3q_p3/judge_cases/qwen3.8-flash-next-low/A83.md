Here's the path: at a sixty million dollar post-money valuation, twenty thousand dollars buys you about 0.0333 percent of the company. Fifty percent cumulative dilution cuts that to roughly 0.0167 percent. At a one billion dollar sale, that share is worth about one hundred sixty-six thousand seven hundred dollars — a multiple of 8.33x, with about 146,700 dollars of profit before taxes.

One assumption worth flagging: this treats the dilution as hitting your stake proportionally, and ignores liquidation preferences. If the later rounds carried preferred stock with a one-times preference, that could take a chunk out of the common-share proceeds before your slice is paid, so the real number could be lower. If those rounds were priced up rounds with plain pro-rata dilution, then 8.33x is the right ballpark.
