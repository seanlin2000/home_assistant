## Consecutive days or Flex?
The standard pass runs on consecutive days: an 8-day pass covers 8 days in a row. The Swiss Travel Pass Flex (from CHF 289) gives you the same number of travel days to use on any dates within one month — you activate each day before your first ride.
## How many days do you need?
- Trip of 3–5 days: a 3 or 4-day pass matching your travel days — or compare with the Half Fare Card, which can win on short trips with few train rides.
- One-week trip: the 8-day pass is the sweet spot — it costs only CHF 40 more than 6 days and covers arrival and departure days, including your airport trains.

## First or second class?
Second class is comfortable, clean and how most Swiss travel — it is the right default. First class buys you quieter cars, wider seats and better odds of a window seat on busy panoramic routes in July–August. If the budget question is “1st class or two extra mountain excursions?”, take the mountains.

Day one, arrive in Lucerne. If you're coming through Zurich airport, the direct train to Lucerne is about fifty minutes and, with the right pass, fully covered. Settle in, and spend the evening by Lake Lucerne. The old town waterfront is flat and walkable, and a sunset from the old bridge is one of the best free views in the country.

Day two, a full day in Lucerne. The Swiss Travel Pass includes lake steamers, so take a cruise on the lake itself — either toward the Flüelen end or a round trip that passes the Weggis promontory, both viewpoints you see from the water with zero effort. In the afternoon, take the cogwheel railway from Alpnachstad up to Mount Pilatus. It's Switzerland's first cog railway, and it delivers you to a high-altitude terrace with panoramic views over the lake and the Alps. The only "hiking" is walking across a level viewing platform.

Day three, the scenic train to Interlaken. The route from Lucerne over the Brünig Pass is a classic — viaducts, forest, mountain lakes — and continues on to Interlaken. In late afternoon, if you're in a hurry, the GoldenPass Express is a more comfortable direct option in about three hours; the older route is more scenic but slower. Interlaken sits between Lake Thun and Lake Brienz, both flat, walkable shores for an evening stroll.

Day four, Interlaken's mountain day, the easiest high view in Switzerland. Take the cogwheel train from Interlaken to Kleine Scheidegg, then the short train ride over to the Eiger, Mönch and Jungfrau station — a viewing platform right beneath the three giants. With the right pass, this whole cogwheel ride is half price, roughly twenty-seven Swiss francs each way instead of fifty-five. That's the most efficient money in the whole trip. If the weather is good and you feel ambitious, the full Jungfraujoch excursion costs more but is also heavily discounted — but for your stated low-effort goal, Kleine Scheidegg is the move.

Day five, Zermatt. From Interlaken, train up the Lötschberg tunnel, out into the Valais, and down the valley to Zermatt in about three and a half hours — the final stretch is a lovely approach to the Matterhorn. In the afternoon, take the Gornergrat cogwheel railway to the 3,089-meter summit. It's the classic Matterhorn viewpoint, the ride itself is fully scenic, and there's a flat, circular trail at the top for photos. A return ticket is roughly 51 francs with the pass discount, versus about 102 full price. If you're short on time, you could do Zermatt as day one instead and finish in Lucerne on the way to the airport.

Day one, arrive in Lucerne. If you're coming through Zurich airport, the direct train to Lucerne is about fifty minutes and, with the right pass, fully covered. Settle in, and spend the evening by Lake Lucerne. The old town waterfront is flat and walkable, and a sunset from the old bridge is one of the best free views in the country.

Day two, a full day in Lucerne. The Swiss Travel Pass includes lake steamers, so take a cruise on the lake itself — either toward the Flüelen end or a round trip that passes the Weggis promontory, both viewpoints you see from the water with zero effort.
