Reference sketch (what a correct answer must contain):
The Hue Bridge is the local hub: it talks to the bulbs over Zigbee and keeps scenes and automations. Share control by inviting the other people in the Hue app or letting them use it on the home network, add physical Hue switches for anyone without a phone, and connect a voice assistant (Apple Home through the bridge, Alexa, Google Home, or a local controller such as Home Assistant). If the internet goes down: the bridge, its automations, the Hue app on the local network and physical switches keep working; Apple Home with a home hub keeps working locally; cloud voice assistants such as Alexa and Google stop taking commands, and control from outside the home stops. A local voice setup avoids that dependency.

## How to restore internet access to the Hue Bridge
### Give the Bridge a fresh DHCP address
A stuck DHCP lease can cause “no internet” even when local control works. In your router admin page, find the DHCP leases list and locate the Hue Bridge entry by its IP or MAC address. Remove the current lease (or “release/clear” it if your router offers that), then restart the Bridge power by unplugging it for 30 seconds. After the Bridge reconnects, it should request a new DHCP lease and receive a valid IP and gateway path. This often resolves DNS/gateway routing issues tied to an old address.

The foundation is what you already have: the Hue Bridge. Wire it to your router with Ethernet, and let all the lights join it over Zigbee. That Zigbee mesh is entirely separate from your Wi-Fi, so the lights don't depend on Wi-Fi at all. For the people in your apartment, don't share your login. In the Hue app, go to Settings, then Home access, and send an invite to each person. If someone moves out, you just remove their access from that same screen.

For voice, the cleanest approach is to link the Bridge to whichever assistants the household actually uses, and Matter makes that multi-ecosystem control officially supported: you can link Apple Home, Google Home, and Alexa to the same Bridge at once, and that's called multi-admin support. So if one person lives on Siri, another on Alexa, and another on Google, all three can voice-control the same lights without anything being shared or duplicated. The one nuance worth knowing: advanced Hue features like gradients still live in the Hue app, so the app stays the "full" control surface and the assistants are a convenient layer on top.

Now the internet-outage part, which is the good news. Because the Bridge and bulbs talk over Zigbee and the Bridge sits on your local network, the Hue app keeps working normally during an outage as long as the phone is on your local network — you just lose remote control from outside the apartment. For voice: with HomeKit or Matter local control, an Apple HomePod or Apple TV on your LAN will keep running your commands without the internet, and Alexa devices with local processing do the same for local devices.
