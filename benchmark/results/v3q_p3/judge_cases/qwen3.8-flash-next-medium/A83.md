ASSISTANT: Here's the math. Your twenty thousand dollars at a sixty million post-money valuation buys you about 0.033 percent of the company. After fifty percent cumulative dilution, that stake is cut in half, to roughly 0.0167 percent.

Applied to a one billion dollar exit, that works out to about $166,667 before taxes. So your money came back 8.33 times over, for a profit of roughly $146,667.

One thing worth noticing: the multiple doesn't depend on how much you invested at all. The exit is sixteen and two thirds times your entry valuation, and the dilution cuts that in half, so anyone who bought in at that sixty million post-money would see the same 8.33 times return. Also worth keeping in mind that "fifty percent cumulative dilution" is doing a lot of work in this scenario, and the real outcome would depend on liquidation preferences from the later rounds, which can take a large bite out of a common shareholder's share before the remainder is split up.
