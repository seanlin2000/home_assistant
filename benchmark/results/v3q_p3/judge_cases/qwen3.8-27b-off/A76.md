Let me separate causes from triggers, because that distinction is where most of the confusion lives.

The underlying causes were structural and decades old. First was economic collapse. The Soviet system had been grinding into decline since the mid-1970s — oil prices kept propping up a system that wasn't actually producing enough, military spending was eating a huge share of the budget to keep pace with the West, and by the 1980s shortages of basic goods were becoming normal. Second was the ideological failure: by the 1980s, ordinary Soviets increasingly didn't believe in the system, and the Communist Party had lost its moral authority. Third was Gorbachev's own reforms — glasnost, meaning openness, and perestroika, meaning restructuring — which were meant to save the system but instead removed the censorship and fear that had held it together. Those are the causes: slow, deep, and building for years.

The three most important events, as immediate triggers, all came in a compressed window from late 1989 to 1991:

One, the fall of the Berlin Wall in November 1989. This was the symbolic breaking point. Once East Germany began opening its borders and millions streamed west, the Soviet satellite states realized Moscow would not send tanks — Gorbachev had explicitly abandoned the Brezhnev Doctrine that said the USSR would use force to keep communist governments in power. Poland, Hungary, Czechoslovakia followed within months. The Eastern Bloc dissolved without a shot fired.

Two, the August 1991 coup attempt against Gorbachev. Hardline communists tried to overthrow him and freeze the reforms. It failed in about three days, but it was decisive: it destroyed what legitimacy the old leadership had left, pushed the republics — especially the Baltic states, Ukraine, and Russia itself — toward outright independence, and made Yeltsin the real power center in Moscow.

Three, the Belavezha Accords of December 1991, when the leaders of Russia, Ukraine, and Belarus declared the Soviet Union effectively dissolved and replaced it with the Commonwealth of Independent States. Gorbachev resigned as president on December 25, 1991, and the USSR was formally gone by early January 1992.
