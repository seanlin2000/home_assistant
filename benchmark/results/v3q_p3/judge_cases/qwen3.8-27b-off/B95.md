**The architecture: five stages in a loop**

Your voice goes through a chain: a wake-word detector listening constantly, then speech detection to find where you start and stop talking, then speech-to-text, then an LLM that thinks and decides what to do, then text-to-speech speaking the answer back. The standard local stack for each stage is openWakeWord for the wake word (runs on CPU alone), Silero VAD for detecting speech boundaries, faster-whisper for transcription, a local LLM like an 8-billion-parameter model running under Ollama, and Piper or Kokoro for the voice — Piper is fast and light, Kokoro sounds noticeably more natural but costs more compute. A thin Python orchestrator glues it together, and Home Assistant sits alongside as the smart-home brain: your LLM turns "turn off the kitchen lights" into a structured command that Home Assistant executes over its local API.

**Hardware for $1,000**

The single most important purchase is an NVIDIA GPU with at least 8 gigabytes of VRAM — a used or new RTX 3060 12GB runs roughly two hundred dollars and is the sweet spot; it comfortably holds an 8B LLM plus Whisper in half-precision. Around that: a mid-range CPU, 32 gigs of RAM (cheap insurance for context windows and Home Assistant), about 500 gigs of SSD since models add up, and a decent USB conference-style microphone array — honestly the mic matters more than people expect, because echo cancellation and pickup quality are what make hands-free conversation possible in a small apartment. That lands you around eight to nine hundred dollars, leaving headroom for a case, power supply, or a second mic for another room. If you'd rather not build a PC, a 16-gig Mac mini is the alternative — it runs the same stack at similar latency — but the GPU box gives more headroom per dollar.

**The biggest limitations, honestly stated**

First, web search: a local LLM knows nothing after its training cutoff, so "answer general questions" really means you need to add a tool-calling step where the model decides to query a search engine and read the results back — that reintroduces the internet for those queries, which is fine but worth acknowledging. Second, conversation memory: keeping multi-turn context costs VRAM, so long conversations degrade or get truncated; this is the main reason your assistant won't feel as smooth as Alexa at chit-chat. Third, smart-home reach: only devices with local APIs work offline — Home Assistant covers a lot, but proprietary ecosystems like Nest or Ring still need their clouds.
