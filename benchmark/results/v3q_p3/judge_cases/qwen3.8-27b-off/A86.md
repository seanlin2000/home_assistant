Reference sketch (what a correct answer must contain):
The Hue Bridge is the local hub: it talks to the bulbs over Zigbee and keeps scenes and automations. Share control by inviting the other people in the Hue app or letting them use it on the home network, add physical Hue switches for anyone without a phone, and connect a voice assistant (Apple Home through the bridge, Alexa, Google Home, or a local controller such as Home Assistant). If the internet goes down: the bridge, its automations, the Hue app on the local network and physical switches keep working; Apple Home with a home hub keeps working locally; cloud voice assistants such as Alexa and Google stop taking commands, and control from outside the home stops. A local voice setup avoids that dependency.

# Exchange 1

USER: I have Philips Hue lights connected to a Hue Bridge. I want multiple people in my apartment to control the lights, including through voice commands. What is the best architecture for this, and what happens if the internet goes down?

ASSISTANT: Here's the setup I'd recommend: put every person's phone on the same Hue account as an "installer" or secondary user in the Philips Hue app, add the Hue Bridge to whichever voice assistant you use — Google Home, Alexa, or Apple HomeKit all work with Hue natively — and make sure each person's voice profile is registered on that assistant. For local control, everyone can use the Hue app directly, which talks to the Bridge over your local Wi-Fi, so no cloud needed for basic on-off and scenes.

The key thing about outages: Hue is one of the better-designed smart home systems here because the Bridge itself handles most logic locally. If the internet drops, the Bridge keeps running, the lights keep responding to commands from the Hue app on your local network, and if you've configured the voice assistant to use local control — Google Home has a "local execution" option, Apple's Siri Shortcuts can run locally, and newer Echo devices with Zigbee or Matter support can handle some commands offline — then voice still works. What dies first is anything that depends on Philips' cloud: remote access from outside the house, complex routines built in the Hue app that sync through their servers, and for Alexa specifically, most voice commands unless you've set up local control explicitly.

So the practical answer: use the Hue app as your primary local fallback, enable local execution in whichever voice assistant you pick, and don't build critical routines solely in the cloud-based parts of the Hue app. If you want me to walk through setting up a specific assistant's local mode, tell me which one you're using.
