USER: How did the Cold War end? Explain the three most important events that led to the dissolution of the Soviet Union, and distinguish between the causes and the immediate triggers.

ASSISTANT: The Cold War ended through a combination of long-term structural decay and a short series of events in 1989 through 1991 that made collapse inevitable once the system could no longer hold itself together.

The three most important events are these.

First, Gorbachev's reforms, glasnost and perestroika, introduced in the mid-1980s. This was the fundamental cause. Glasnost loosened state control over information, which let people in the Soviet republics air grievances that had been suppressed for decades. Perestroika attempted to restructure the economy but ended up removing the central planning controls that, however inefficient, had kept the system functioning. Gorbachev didn't want to break up the Union, but his reforms made it impossible for the center to control the periphery by force without looking ridiculous.

Second, the revolutions of 1989 across Eastern Europe, culminating in the fall of the Berlin Wall in November. This was both a consequence of the first cause and a new shock in itself. When Poland, Hungary, Czechoslovakia, and Romania all changed governments within months, and when the Berlin Wall came down, it proved that the Soviet system could not maintain its sphere of influence. It also shifted the crisis inward: now the question was no longer whether the satellites would break free, but whether the Union itself would hold.

Third, the August 1991 coup attempt. Hardliners in the government and security services tried to arrest Gorbachev and reverse the reforms. The coup failed within three days, and that failure was the immediate trigger for dissolution. Before the coup, there was still a central government, however weak. After it, the republics saw that the center was finished. Within weeks, the Baltic states declared full independence, and in December the leaders of Russia, Ukraine, and Belarus signed the Belavezha Accords, formally declaring the Soviet Union dissolved and replacing it with the Commonwealth of Independent States. Gorbachev resigned on December twenty-fifth.

So to draw the line the question asks for: the cause was the structural one — an economy that had been stagnating since the late seventies, an empire too expensive to maintain, and a reform program that inadvertently removed the tools of control. The immediate trigger was the failed coup, because it destroyed whatever authority remained at the center and gave every republic the green light to leave. Without the coup, the Union might have limped on in some degraded form for another year or two. With it, the process that had been unfolding for a decade compressed into a matter of weeks.
