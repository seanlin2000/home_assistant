Reference sketch (what a correct answer must contain):
Sunlight holds every color. Air molecules scatter short wavelengths (blue) far more than long ones (red), about the inverse fourth power of wavelength (Rayleigh scattering), so blue light bounces around the sky and reaches you from every direction. The sky is not violet because the sun gives off less violet, some is absorbed high up, and eyes are less sensitive to it. At sunset the light crosses far more air on its long, low path, so most of the blue is scattered out before it reaches you and the reds and oranges are left; dust and haze strengthen the effect. # Exchange 1

USER: Why is the sky blue during the day but red and orange during sunset? Explain the physics in a way that someone without a science background can understand.

ASSISTANT: Sunlight looks white, but it is really a mix of every color, and the colors behave differently when they bump into the tiny gas molecules in the air. Those molecules are much smaller than the waves of light, and they scatter short waves, blue and violet, far more strongly than long waves like red. So during the day, blue light gets bounced all over the sky and reaches your eyes from every direction, which is why the whole sky looks blue. It is not violet mostly because the sun sends out less violet, some is absorbed high up, and our eyes are more sensitive to blue. At sunset the sun is low, so its light has to travel through much more air to reach you. Along that long path, most of the blue gets scattered off to the side before it ever arrives. What is left is the reds, oranges and yellows, and dust, smoke or haze can make them even deeper.
