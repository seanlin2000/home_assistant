Reference sketch (what a correct answer must contain):
The stock market reflects expected profits of large, often multinational companies, and stock ownership is concentrated (in the US the richest tenth of households own the large majority of stocks), while people feel the economy through wages, prices, rent and jobs. Three concrete examples from any of: prices rising faster than wages while companies keep or raise margins; a handful of giant tech firms driving index gains while most businesses and regions lag; companies earning much of their profit abroad; cost cutting and layoffs that raise profits and share prices; low rates or stimulus inflating asset prices, which helps people who own assets (the K-shaped recovery of 2020 and 2021); housing costs climbing while the index climbs. Must give three distinct, concrete mechanisms, not three restatements of "the market is not the economy".

# Exchange 1

USER: Why can a country have a strong stock market while its average citizens feel like the economy is getting worse? Give three concrete examples of how this happens.

ASSISTANT: Because the stock market and an ordinary person's wallet measure different things. Stocks track the expected future profits of big public companies, and most shares are owned by the wealthiest households, so a rising market mostly lifts people who already have assets. Three concrete ways the gap opens up. First, profits can rise while wages lag: if companies hold prices high or cut labor costs, profit margins grow and share prices climb, while paychecks fall behind rent and groceries. Second, inflation and interest rates hit people unevenly: a renter carrying a credit card balance feels higher prices and borrowing costs right away, while an investor's portfolio can keep rising on good earnings news. Third, the market is concentrated and global: a handful of giant tech companies can carry the whole index on AI spending or overseas sales, even as local hiring, small businesses and housing affordability get worse. On top of that, markets look forward, betting on future earnings, while people feel today's costs, so the two can drift apart for years.
