Choppy audio: the speaker may also be paired with the laptop, so the laptop competes for the connection (multipoint); 2.4 GHz interference from Wi-Fi and the laptop's own Bluetooth; distance or obstacles; a weak Bluetooth chip or codec in the projector. Steps, one change at a time: turn off the laptop's Bluetooth or unpair the speaker from it, move the devices closer, put the laptop on 5 GHz Wi-Fi, update the firmware, and test a wired audio link or a low-latency transmitter. # Exchange 1

USER: My projector is connected to a JBL Bluetooth speaker. Sometimes the audio becomes choppy when I connect my laptop, and playing video games on the projector causes more eye strain than playing on my monitor. What could cause these problems, and how would you systematically troubleshoot them?

ASSISTANT: Those are probably two separate issues. For choppy audio, the usual causes are Bluetooth interference and bandwidth. Bluetooth shares the crowded two point four gigahertz band with Wi-Fi, so a router, a laptop radio or other devices can interrupt it. The laptop may also be using a weaker codec, or sending video and audio across a busy Wi-Fi link at once. Distance or obstructions between the speaker and the laptop, and a low battery on the speaker, also matter. Troubleshoot by changing one thing at a time: move the speaker closer with a clear line of sight, switch the router to the five gigahertz band or move it away, disconnect other Bluetooth devices, update the laptop's Bluetooth and audio drivers, and test with another source such as your phone. For eye strain, a projector image is typically dimmer and lower contrast than a monitor, and playing in a dark room makes the screen far brighter than its surroundings. Input lag, low refresh rate, blur, and fixed focus issues also tire the eyes. Try some room lighting, check focus and brightness settings, enable a game mode, and take breaks.
