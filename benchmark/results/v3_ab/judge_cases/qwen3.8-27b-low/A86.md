# Question A86 (category A)
What it tests: Smart-home architecture, local versus cloud control
Expected search behavior: no
Expected reply: an ordinary answer

Reference sketch (what a correct answer must contain):
The Hue Bridge is the local hub: it talks to the bulbs over Zigbee and keeps scenes and automations. Share control by inviting the other people in the Hue app or letting them use it on the home network, add physical Hue switches for anyone without a phone, and connect a voice assistant (Apple Home through the bridge, Alexa, Google Home, or a local controller such as Home Assistant). If the internet goes down: the bridge, its automations, the Hue app on the local network and physical switches keep working; Apple Home with a home hub keeps working locally; cloud voice assistants such as Alexa and Google stop taking commands, and control from outside the home stops. A local voice setup avoids that dependency.

# Exchange 1

USER: I have Philips Hue lights connected to a Hue Bridge. I want multiple people in my apartment to control the lights, including through voice commands. What is the best architecture for this, and what happens if the internet goes down?

ASSISTANT: The good news is that Philips Hue is already designed around this. The bridge is a local Zigbee hub, which means the lights talk to the bridge over a radio frequency that has nothing to do with the internet. As long as your phone or tablet is on your home WiFi, the Hue app talks directly to the bridge on your local network, so multiple people can each install the app, pair with the bridge, and control the lights independently, all without any cloud involvement.

For voice, the practical path is to connect the bridge to a voice assistant like Alexa or Google Home. Each person can then use whatever speaker they have, and the assistant sends commands to the bridge over your local network. That works fine as long as the internet is up.

Here is the important part about an internet outage. The lights and the bridge keep working together because Zigbee is entirely local. The Hue app on your phone still works because it is talking to the bridge on your LAN. But the voice assistants stop working, because the speech recognition and command processing happens on their cloud servers, not on the speaker itself. So you would lose voice control during an outage, but you would not lose light control. You could still use the app, or any physical Hue wall switch or smart plug you have.

If offline voice control is a real requirement for you, the option is to run Home Assistant as a local server on your network. It can talk to the Hue bridge directly, and it supports a local voice pipeline, meaning a microphone, speech-to-text, and text-to-speech all running on your own hardware, with no cloud. That is significantly more work to set up and maintain, but it is the only way to get voice commands that survive a full internet outage with Hue lights.

So the short version: for most people, the bridge plus the Hue app for each person plus a mainstream voice assistant is the right setup, with the understanding that voice goes away but the lights do not. If you want voice to survive an outage, Home Assistant is the path, but budget a real weekend for the setup.
