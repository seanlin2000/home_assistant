USER: How did the Cold War end? Explain the three most important events that led to the dissolution of the Soviet Union, and distinguish between the causes and the immediate triggers.

ASSISTANT: The short version: the Cold War ended because the Soviet system ran out of money, legitimacy, and control at the same time, and the final collapse came in a chain reaction across Eastern Europe that Moscow could no longer stop with force. Historians debate the exact recipe, but the three events I'd single out are Gorbachev's reforms, the fall of the Berlin Wall, and the August 1991 coup attempt.

The first is Gorbachev coming to power in 1985 and launching perestroika and glasnost. This is a cause rather than a trigger: by the mid-eighties the Soviet economy was stagnating, the arms race with Reagan's administration was draining the treasury, and Afghanistan was a quagmire. Gorbachev tried to fix it from inside by loosening political controls and opening up the economy. The problem was that glasnost exposed how badly things were actually working, which destroyed the Communist Party's claim to legitimacy faster than perestroika could deliver improvements. So this event set everything in motion, but it wasn't the end itself.

The second is the fall of the Berlin Wall on November 9th, 1989, and the wave of revolutions across Eastern Europe that winter. This is where cause becomes trigger. Gorbachev's explicit refusal to repeat Hungary in '56 or Czechoslovakia in '68 — what's called the Sinatra Doctrine, Poland doing it "its own way" — removed the threat of Soviet tanks from every country in the bloc. East Germany was just the most dramatic case: a botched press conference about travel rules turned into hundreds of thousands at the wall, and the guards opened it. Once that happened, Hungary, Czechoslovakia, Romania followed within months. For the USSR this was the point where the buffer zone that had defined its security since 1945 simply evaporated.

The third is the August 1991 coup attempt by hardline communists who tried to overthrow Gorbachev. This is the immediate trigger for the actual dissolution, and it's worth separating carefully from the causes. The coup lasted about three days and failed, largely because Boris Yeltsin in Russia openly defied it, standing on a tank outside the Russian parliament building in Moscow. But its failure was fatal to the old order in two ways: it discredited the Communist Party of the Soviet Union so thoroughly that Gorbachev suspended its activities, and it transferred real power from the Soviet center to the individual republics, led by Yeltsin. After that, there was effectively no central authority left capable of holding the union together.
