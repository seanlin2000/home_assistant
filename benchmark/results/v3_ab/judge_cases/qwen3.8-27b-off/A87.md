ASSISTANT: Let me think through both problems for you.

**The choppy audio when your laptop joins**

A few likely culprits:

1. **Bluetooth bandwidth is shared.** If your laptop and projector are both streaming Bluetooth audio to the same JBL speaker (or if the speaker is bonded to two devices), it has to time-share between them. That alone can cause stuttering, especially if one device is playing something at the same time as the other.

2. **The speaker is bonded to multiple sources.** Most JBL speakers let you pair with more than one device and will switch or mix between them. When your laptop connects, the speaker may be trying to handle both streams at once.

3. **Interference or distance.** A laptop's Wi-Fi (especially 2.4 GHz) sits in the same band as Bluetooth. If the laptop is near the projector or the speaker, you can get packet loss and choppy audio.

**Systematic troubleshooting for the audio:**
- First, unpair your laptop from the JBL speaker entirely, then test with only the projector connected. If the choppiness goes away, the multi-device bonding is the cause. You can then decide whether to keep both paired (accepting occasional stutter) or disconnect one at a time.
- If it still stutters with just the projector, move the speaker closer to the projector and farther from your laptop's Wi-Fi radio, and try switching the laptop's Wi-Fi to 5 GHz if your router supports it.
- Check the JBL app or manual for a "dual connect" or "multi-point" setting — turning that off forces one device at a time.
- Make sure the speaker firmware is up to date; JBL has released fixes for exactly this kind of stuttering.

**The eye strain from the projector vs. the monitor**

This is very common, and it's not your imagination. A few reasons:

1. **Lower effective resolution.** Even a 1080p or 4K projector often displays at a lower native resolution than a modern laptop or desktop monitor. Text edges get softer, and your eyes work harder to resolve them.

2. **Ambient light mismatch.** Projectors need a dimmer room, so you're likely playing in lower light with a brighter-than-necessary image, or in a room that's too bright for the projector to hit good contrast. Both increase strain.

3. **Refresh rate and motion clarity.** Many projectors run at 60 Hz but with more input lag and less motion handling than a dedicated gaming monitor. That extra lag and blur make your eyes track moving objects harder.

4. **Screen size and distance.** A projected image is large, so you're scanning more visual field per frame, which tires the eyes faster during long sessions.
