Take the short lake steamer from Lucerne's pier to Vitznau or Weggis on Lake Lucerne, both charming little villages you can wander for an hour or two with no walking required beyond flat paths along the shore. In the afternoon, ride the cog railway up Mount Pilatus, which is the classic choice because it goes by train and cable car rather than on foot, and the summit offers a full panorama of the lake and the surrounding peaks. Pilatus is the lower effort option; Rigi nearby works too if you prefer its gentler ridge walk.

Day two, Lucerne to Interlaken. This is one of the great rides in Switzerland, the line from Lucerne through Brunnen and along Lake Brienz to Interlaken, with the lake on your left for much of the way. Make it a proper journey rather than a race: take an early morning train, stop at Brienz if you want to see the famous turquoise water up close, and let the train take you in by mid afternoon. From there the town sits between Lake Thun and Lake Brienz, so you can spend the evening lakeside with zero effort.

Day three, Interlaken viewpoints. The easiest big mountain day here is Jungfraujoch, the top of Europe, reached entirely by train: a standard line up to Kleine Scheidegg, then a cog railway through the mountain to the summit station at about 3,450 meters. It is cold and you should buy a warm layer, but there is no hiking involved, just walking on flat, snow packed platforms with incredible views of the Eiger, Mönch and Jungfrau. If weather closes it, the fallback is Grindelwald First or Männlichen, both cable car rides with short optional boardwalks rather than real trails.

Day four, Interlaken to Zermatt. This is the scenic highlight of the whole trip and worth planning around. The line runs from Interlaken through the Lötschberg base tunnel and out into the Rhône valley to Spiez, then up past Visp into the Mattertal with the Matterhorn rising ahead of you as you reach Zermatt. It is a long day, roughly four to five hours door to door, so leave in the morning and enjoy it as a sightseeing ride rather than something to rush. Zermatt itself is car free and flat enough to stroll on foot or by the little electric taxis, and the first look of the Matterhorn from the station is one of those moments you will remember.
