Expected search behavior: no

Reference sketch (what a correct answer must contain):
The Hue Bridge is the local hub: it talks to the bulbs over Zigbee and keeps scenes and automations. Share control by inviting the other people in the Hue app or letting them use it on the home network, add physical Hue switches for anyone without a phone, and connect a voice assistant (Apple Home through the bridge, Alexa, Google Home, or a local controller such as Home Assistant). If the internet goes down: the bridge, its automations, the Hue app on the local network and physical switches keep working; Apple Home with a home hub keeps working locally; cloud voice assistants such as Alexa and Google stop taking commands, and control from outside the home stops. A local voice setup avoids that dependency.

# Exchange 1

USER: I have Philips Hue lights connected to a Hue Bridge. I want multiple people in my apartment to control the lights, including through voice commands. What is the best architecture for this, and what happens if the internet goes down?

ASSISTANT: Here's how I'd set it up. Everyone on your home Wi-Fi network adds the Hue Bridge to their own Philips Hue app using the four-digit code from the back of the bridge. Once the bridge is shared that way, each person gets full local control over the lights, and no one has to rely on anyone else's phone or account. For voice, if you use Apple HomeKit, Google Home, or Alexa, the Hue Bridge can be connected directly to each assistant locally. The important part is that the lights themselves stay on your home network through the bridge, so day to day control does not need the internet at all.

The main caveat is cloud dependency. If your internet goes down, local control keeps working: people in the apartment can still change the lights with their phones and with voice commands as long as the smart speaker or hub is on your local network. What stops working is anything that needs Philips's servers, like controlling the lights from outside your home, updating firmware, or certain cloud-based routines. Also, if you set up scenes or automations through a third-party assistant that routes through its own cloud, those specific voice commands may fail without internet, while the basic Hue local commands and app control continue to work fine. So for your use case, sharing the bridge across everyone's phones plus local voice assistants gives you solid multi-person control with graceful degradation when the internet drops.
