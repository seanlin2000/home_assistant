Reference sketch (what a correct answer must contain):
The Hue Bridge is the local hub: it talks to the bulbs over Zigbee and keeps scenes and automations. Share control by inviting the other people in the Hue app or letting them use it on the home network, add physical Hue switches for anyone without a phone, and connect a voice assistant (Apple Home through the bridge, Alexa, Google Home, or a local controller such as Home Assistant). If the internet goes down: the bridge, its automations, the Hue app on the local network and physical switches keep working; Apple Home with a home hub keeps working locally; cloud voice assistants such as Alexa and Google stop taking commands, and control from outside the home stops. A local voice setup avoids that dependency.

# Exchange 1

USER: I have Philips Hue lights connected to a Hue Bridge. I want multiple people in my apartment to control the lights, including through voice commands. What is the best architecture for this, and what happens if the internet goes down?

ASSISTANT: The best setup is to keep the Hue Bridge on your home network and control it through a hub that supports Matter or the Hue integration, for example Apple Home, Google Home, Amazon Alexa, or Home Assistant. Add everyone in your apartment to the same home in that app, so they can all use the app and voice assistants like Siri, Alexa, or Google. The Hue app can also invite other users through a Hue account. If you want voice commands that work even offline, a local hub like Home Assistant with a local voice setup is the most reliable. If the internet goes down, it depends. The lights and the bridge still work on your local network, so the physical Hue dimmer switches and Hue accessories keep working, and apps that talk to the bridge locally, such as Home Assistant, Apple Home, and the Hue app on the same Wi-Fi, still work. Cloud-based voice commands, such as Alexa and Google, usually stop, since the voice recognition happens online. Remote control from outside the apartment also stops.
