Reference sketch (what a correct answer must contain):
The Hue Bridge is the local hub: it talks to the bulbs over Zigbee and keeps scenes and automations. Share control by inviting the other people in the Hue app or letting them use it on the home network, add physical Hue switches for anyone without a phone, and connect a voice assistant (Apple Home through the bridge, Alexa, Google Home, or a local controller such as Home Assistant). If the internet goes down: the bridge, its automations, the Hue app on the local network and physical switches keep working; Apple Home with a home hub keeps working locally; cloud voice assistants such as Alexa and Google stop taking commands, and control from outside the home stops. A local voice setup avoids that dependency.

# Exchange 1

USER: I have Philips Hue lights connected to a Hue Bridge. I want multiple people in my apartment to control the lights, including through voice commands. What is the best architecture for this, and what happens if the internet goes down?

ASSISTANT: Keep the Hue Bridge as the single hub, since it talks to the bulbs locally over Zigbee, and give everyone ways in through it. For people, invite housemates to your Hue home in the app, or let them use the app on your Wi-Fi, so everyone controls the same lights. For voice, link the bridge to the assistant your household prefers: Alexa, Google Home, or Apple Home through the bridge's HomeKit or Matter support. Add a Hue dimmer switch or two so guests need no app at all. For the most robust setup, run Home Assistant on a small always-on computer; it talks to the bridge locally, gives everyone one shared dashboard, and can handle voice locally. If the internet goes down, the bridge keeps working on your home network: the app on Wi-Fi, physical switches, schedules and automations on the bridge, and Home Assistant all keep going. You lose control from outside the home, and Alexa and Google voice commands fail because they depend on the cloud. Apple Home with a home hub usually keeps working locally.
