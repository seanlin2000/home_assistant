Choppy audio: the speaker may also be paired with the laptop, so the laptop competes for the connection (multipoint); 2.4 GHz interference from Wi-Fi and the laptop's own Bluetooth; distance or obstacles; a weak Bluetooth chip or codec in the projector. Steps, one change at a time: turn off the laptop's Bluetooth or unpair the speaker from it, move the devices closer, put the laptop on 5 GHz Wi-Fi, update the firmware, and test a wired audio link or a low-latency transmitter. Eye strain: brightness against room light, an image that fills more of the field of view so the eyes and head move more, sitting too close, soft focus or keystone distortion, motion blur or input lag, and with DLP projectors the rainbow effect from the color wheel; also blinking less during games. # Exchange 1

USER: My projector is connected to a JBL Bluetooth speaker. Sometimes the audio becomes choppy when I connect my laptop, and playing video games on the projector causes more eye strain than playing on my monitor. What could cause these problems, and how would you systematically troubleshoot them?

ASSISTANT: These are probably two separate problems. Choppy audio usually means Bluetooth interference or a tug-of-war over the speaker. Bluetooth shares the crowded two point four gigahertz band with Wi-Fi, so when the laptop connects, its traffic can crowd the signal, or the laptop may grab the speaker itself if it was paired before. Change one thing at a time: unpair the speaker from the laptop, move your Wi-Fi to five gigahertz, shorten the distance between projector and speaker, and test with the laptop's Wi-Fi off. If it persists, use a cable from the projector's audio out, which also cuts lag. The eye strain likely comes from a dim image in a bright room or a harsh one in a dark room, a picture too big for your seating distance, slightly soft focus or keystone correction, a lower refresh rate or more motion blur than your monitor, or the rainbow flicker some single-chip projectors cause. Test by adjusting room lighting, turning on game mode, refocusing, sitting farther back, and taking breaks.
