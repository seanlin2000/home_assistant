USER: Imagine I want to build a private, locally hosted voice assistant that can answer general questions, search the web, control smart-home devices, and have natural conversations. I have a $1,000 hardware budget. Propose an architecture, explain the components, identify the biggest limitations, and prioritize what I should build first.

ASSISTANT: Here's a solid plan for about a thousand dollars. The core is one always-on computer: a mini PC or used desktop with a GPU of around sixteen gigabytes of video memory, or an Apple Silicon Mac mini with as much memory as you can afford. On it, run Home Assistant for smart-home control, Whisper for speech-to-text, a local language model of roughly seven to fourteen billion parameters through llama.cpp or Ollama, and Piper for a natural voice. Connect them through Home Assistant's Wyoming protocol, and give the model web search, page fetching and device control as tools through an MCP server. Then add a few cheap satellite microphones with wake-word detection. The biggest limitations are that a small local model reasons and recalls facts worse than cloud models, delay adds up across listening, thinking and speaking, cheap microphones struggle across a room, and web search still exposes your queries to the provider. Build in this order: Home Assistant with device control first, then local voice in one room, then the language model, then web search, and finally more satellites and polish.
