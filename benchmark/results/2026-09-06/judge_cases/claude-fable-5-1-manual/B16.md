ASSISTANT: Based on the market data I found, a four percent annual increase looks a bit steep for Long Island City right now. The median asking rent is about forty five hundred a month as of June, which is roughly fifty five dollars below the peak set last September, so asking rents have been flat to slightly down over the past year. If your starting rent is at market, four percent a year is above what the market itself has done. That said, be careful with that comparison. Asking rents are what landlords hope for, not what leases sign at, and they hide concessions like free months. The median also shifts with what is listed, and Long Island City has added thousands of new luxury units, which pull the number up without your building changing. Rents are also seasonal, most leases sign in summer when asking prices peak. The better comparison is what similar units in your building actually rented for, and whether your current rent is already below market. A two year lease at four percent is worth it mainly if it locks in a below market starting rent.
