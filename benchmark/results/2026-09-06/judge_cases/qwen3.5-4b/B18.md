ASSISTANT: Based on current data for the Qwen family, you can comfortably run dense models up to **Qwen3-8B** in full precision or quantized versions (like Q4_K_M) on a single 16GB GPU. For higher quality, the **Qwen2.5-7B** or **Qwen3-14B** (heavily quantized) are your realistic options, though the latter will be tight and slower.

Here is how the tradeoffs break down:

**Memory Usage vs. Quality:**
The most obvious constraint is VRAM. A full-precision 14B model requires roughly 28GB just for weights, which means it will not fit on a 16GB card regardless of quantization unless you are willing to offload massive portions of the context or use extremely aggressive quantization that sacrifices quality. The 32B dense model needs at least 24GB VRAM even with INT4 quantization, and that is still very tight for anything but short prompts because generation overhead eats up memory quickly.

**Quantization Strategies:**
To get more parameters into your 16GB box, you rely on quantization—reducing the precision of weights from 16-bit floating point to 4-bit integers. The industry standard for a good balance is **Q4_K_M**, which offers "good" quality with decent compression. If you push to **Q3** or **Q2**, you can fit larger models (potentially up to a 20B range), but the "quality cliff" becomes noticeable, especially in reasoning and instruction following. FP8 quantization is newer and more efficient for large models like Qwen3-32B, but it requires specific hardware support or conversion tools that aren't universally available on consumer GPUs.

**Speed Considerations:**
Inference speed is almost entirely bound by memory bandwidth, not compute power. A larger model will simply be slower to generate tokens, regardless of whether you have a fast GPU. The **Qwen3-8B** at Q4 will likely give you the best experience: high token generation speed (roughly 30+ tokens per second on mid-range GPUs), reasonable context handling for most tasks, and full compatibility with existing quantized tools like llama.cpp or Ollama.

**Mixture of Experts (MoE) Models:**
Newer MoE models like Qwen3-30B-A3B are interesting because they activate only a fraction of their parameters per token, making them compute-efficient. However, they do not save memory; you still need to load all 30 billion parameters into VRAM at once. This means they require the same amount of RAM as non-MoE models of that total size, negating their local-running advantage unless you have multi-GPU setups.
