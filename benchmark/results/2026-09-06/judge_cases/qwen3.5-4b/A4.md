USER: My plant is growing new leaves quickly near the top, but its older lower leaves are gradually yellowing and dying. The plant is otherwise healthy. What are the most likely explanations, and what information would you want before deciding whether this is normal or a problem?

ASSISTANT: The pattern you're describing—new growth at the top is vigorous while old leaves at the bottom yellow—is actually quite common for many houseplants and often indicates a few specific things depending on the species. The most likely explanations are that the plant is naturally senescing its lower leaves to recycle nutrients upward, which can be completely normal, or it could be a sign of root-bound status where the plant is redirecting energy away from older roots as they age out of the soil volume. Other possibilities include underwatering if the lower leaves have had less time to acclimate to dry conditions, or perhaps light competition if this was a recent transplant and the new growth is establishing itself in the best light available. Before I can decide if this is normal behavior for your specific plant, I would need to know what type of plant it is, since some species naturally drop bottom leaves more than others; whether you recently repotted or moved it; how consistently you are watering; and what kind of light it's receiving.
