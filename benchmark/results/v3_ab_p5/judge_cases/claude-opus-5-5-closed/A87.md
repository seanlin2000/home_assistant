Choppy audio: the speaker may also be paired with the laptop, so the laptop competes for the connection (multipoint); 2.4 GHz interference from Wi-Fi and the laptop's own Bluetooth; distance or obstacles; a weak Bluetooth chip or codec in the projector. # Exchange 1

USER: My projector is connected to a JBL Bluetooth speaker. Sometimes the audio becomes choppy when I connect my laptop, and playing video games on the projector causes more eye strain than playing on my monitor. What could cause these problems, and how would you systematically troubleshoot them?

ASSISTANT: For choppy audio, the likely cause is the Bluetooth link. Many JBL speakers pair with two sources, so your laptop may grab or share the connection with the projector, and Bluetooth shares the two point four gigahertz band with Wi-Fi, often on the laptop's same antenna. Change one thing at a time: unpair the laptop from the speaker and see if it stops, move the speaker closer with a clear line of sight, put your Wi-Fi on five gigahertz, and if possible run an audio cable, which also removes the Bluetooth lag that hurts gaming. For eye strain, suspects are a picture too bright for a dark room, a screen too big or close, soft focus or digital keystone blurring the image, a low refresh rate, and on single-chip DLP projectors, the rainbow effect from the color wheel. Turn on game mode, match the projector's native resolution, square up the projector physically instead of using keystone, add some room light, sit farther back, and notice whether you see color flashes when your eyes move.
