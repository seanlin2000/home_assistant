Reference sketch (what a correct answer must contain):
A stock is worth the present value of its future cash flows, discounted at a rate that builds on the risk-free rate. When the Fed raises rates the discount rate rises, and cash flows far in the future lose much more present value than near ones (as with long-dated bonds). Growth and technology companies get most of their value from profits expected years ahead, so they are long-duration assets and their valuations fall more. Also: bonds become a more attractive alternative, borrowing costs rise for unprofitable companies, and valuation multiples compress (2022, when the Nasdaq fell about a third, is the standard example). A worked figure helps but is optional: 100 dollars due in 10 years is worth about 74 today at 3 percent and about 56 at 6 percent.

# Exchange 1

USER: Why does the Federal Reserve raising interest rates generally put downward pressure on technology stock valuations? Explain the relationship between interest rates, discounted cash flows, and long-duration growth stocks.

ASSISTANT: Rising rates hurt tech stocks mainly because of how investors value future money. A stock is worth, in theory, all the cash the company will ever pay out, with each future dollar discounted back to today. The discount rate starts from the risk-free return on Treasury bonds, so when the Fed raises rates, that baseline goes up, and every future dollar is worth less today. The key is timing. A utility or a bank earns most of its value in the next few years, so a higher discount rate trims it only a little. A fast-growing tech company is valued mostly on profits expected ten or twenty years from now, and discounting compounds over each year, so those distant dollars shrink much more. That's why growth stocks are called long-duration assets: like long-term bonds, their prices are most sensitive to rates. Higher rates add other pressures too. Safe bonds now pay a decent return, so investors need more reward to take risk, and unprofitable companies that rely on borrowing or raising capital find money more expensive.
