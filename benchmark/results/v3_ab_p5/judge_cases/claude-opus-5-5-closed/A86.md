Reference sketch (what a correct answer must contain):
The Hue Bridge is the local hub: it talks to the bulbs over Zigbee and keeps scenes and automations. Share control by inviting the other people in the Hue app or letting them use it on the home network, add physical Hue switches for anyone without a phone, and connect a voice assistant (Apple Home through the bridge, Alexa, Google Home, or a local controller such as Home Assistant). If the internet goes down: the bridge, its automations, the Hue app on the local network and physical switches keep working; Apple Home with a home hub keeps working locally; cloud voice assistants such as Alexa and Google stop taking commands, and control from outside the home stops. A local voice setup avoids that dependency.

# Exchange 1

USER: I have Philips Hue lights connected to a Hue Bridge. I want multiple people in my apartment to control the lights, including through voice commands. What is the best architecture for this, and what happens if the internet goes down?

ASSISTANT: Keep the Hue Bridge at the center, since it controls the bulbs locally over Zigbee and everything else talks to it. Invite each household member into your Hue home through the Hue app so everyone has their own login, and put a Hue dimmer switch or smart button by the door for guests. For voice, Alexa and Google Assistant are easy to share but run through their clouds. The most resilient option is Home Assistant connected to the bridge, with its local voice assistant and a speaker satellite, so speech recognition happens on your own network. Apple Home through a HomePod is a good middle ground for iPhone households. If the internet goes down, the bridge, bulbs, switches, and schedules stored on the bridge keep working, and the Hue app still works on home Wi-Fi. Home Assistant's local voice keeps working too. What stops is control from outside the apartment and cloud voice assistants like Alexa and Google, and Siri may struggle as well. If the router itself fails, phones lose the bridge, and only the physical switches remain.
