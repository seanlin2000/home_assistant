Reference sketch (what a correct answer must contain):
Choppy audio: the speaker may also be paired with the laptop, so the laptop competes for the connection (multipoint); 2.4 GHz interference from Wi-Fi and the laptop's own Bluetooth; distance or obstacles; a weak Bluetooth chip or codec in the projector. Steps, one change at a time: turn off the laptop's Bluetooth or unpair the speaker from it, move the devices closer, put the laptop on 5 GHz Wi-Fi, update the firmware, and test a wired audio link or a low-latency transmitter. Eye strain: brightness against room light, an image that fills more of the field of view so the eyes and head move more, sitting too close, soft focus or keystone distortion, motion blur or input lag, and with DLP projectors the rainbow effect from the color wheel; also blinking less during games. Suggestions: a darker room or balanced brightness, a game mode, correct focus and seating distance, and breaks. Should separate the two problems and give an order of tests.

# Exchange 1

USER: My projector is connected to a JBL Bluetooth speaker. Sometimes the audio becomes choppy when I connect my laptop, and playing video games on the projector causes more eye strain than playing on my monitor. What could cause these problems, and how would you systematically troubleshoot them?

ASSISTANT: For the choppy audio, the likeliest cause is Bluetooth interference or bandwidth. Your laptop's Bluetooth and Wi-Fi both use the crowded 2.4 gigahertz band, and the projector may be broadcasting too, so a second connection can drop packets. Distance, walls and a weak laptop Bluetooth chip can also matter. To troubleshoot, test one change at a time. Connect the speaker to the laptop alone, then add the projector. Move the devices closer, switch the laptop's Wi-Fi to the 5 gigahertz band, and update drivers. If a wired option exists, try a cable or a Bluetooth adapter. For eye strain, compare to your monitor on these points. A projector is usually dimmer in contrast, may be too bright in a dark room, or too dim in a lit room. Resolution, focus, a low refresh rate, input lag settings and flicker can also tire the eyes. Check the focus, brightness and gamma settings, turn on the game mode, match the room lighting, sit farther back, and take breaks. Change one setting at a time and see what helps.
