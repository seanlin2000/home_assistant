ASSISTANT: The Cold War ended between nineteen eighty-nine and nineteen ninety-one, largely peacefully, as the Soviet bloc came apart from within. The first key event was Mikhail Gorbachev's reform drive after nineteen eighty-five: glasnost and perestroika loosened censorship and central control, which let long-buried grievances surface and weakened the Communist Party's grip. The second was the wave of revolutions in Eastern Europe in nineteen eighty-nine, symbolized by the fall of the Berlin Wall, when Moscow chose not to send in tanks and the satellite governments fell one after another. The third was the failed hardline coup of August nineteen ninety-one, which discredited the old guard, lifted Boris Yeltsin, and sped the republics toward independence, until the union dissolved that December. The causes were long-running: a stagnant command economy, the cost of the arms race and the war in Afghanistan, falling oil revenue, and nationalism in the republics. The immediate triggers were the moments that turned slow decay into collapse: the Wall opening, the coup's failure, and the December agreement in which Russia, Ukraine and Belarus declared the union finished.
