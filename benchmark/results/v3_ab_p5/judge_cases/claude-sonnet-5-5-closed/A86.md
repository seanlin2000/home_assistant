Reference sketch (what a correct answer must contain):
The Hue Bridge is the local hub: it talks to the bulbs over Zigbee and keeps scenes and automations. Share control by inviting the other people in the Hue app or letting them use it on the home network, add physical Hue switches for anyone without a phone, and connect a voice assistant (Apple Home through the bridge, Alexa, Google Home, or a local controller such as Home Assistant). If the internet goes down: the bridge, its automations, the Hue app on the local network and physical switches keep working; Apple Home with a home hub keeps working locally; cloud voice assistants such as Alexa and Google stop taking commands, and control from outside the home stops. A local voice setup avoids that dependency.

# Exchange 1

USER: I have Philips Hue lights connected to a Hue Bridge. I want multiple people in my apartment to control the lights, including through voice commands. What is the best architecture for this, and what happens if the internet goes down?

ASSISTANT: The best setup is to keep the Hue Bridge as the core, since it talks to the lights locally over Zigbee, and then add control layers on top. For multiple people, give each person the Hue app, so they can control lights on your home network, and for voice commands, link the bridge to Amazon Alexa, Google Home or Apple HomeKit. Hue Bridge supports all three. With HomeKit or Google Home, you can invite other household members to share control. Physical Hue dimmer switches or smart buttons are also good, since guests can use them without a phone. If you want a fully local system, you can use Home Assistant, which connects to the bridge over your local network and can offer a local voice assistant. If the internet goes down, the bridge and lights still work locally. Wall switches, dimmer switches, schedules and automations stored on the bridge keep running, and the Hue app and HomeKit work over Wi-Fi on the home network. What usually fails is cloud-based voice, like Alexa or Google, and remote control while you are away. Keep the bridge on Ethernet and give it a fixed address.
