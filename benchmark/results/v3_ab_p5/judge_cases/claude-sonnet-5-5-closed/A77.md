# Exchange 1

USER: Why is the sky blue during the day but red and orange during sunset? Explain the physics in a way that someone without a science background can understand.

ASSISTANT: Sunlight looks white, but it is really a mix of all the colors. When it passes through the air, the tiny gas molecules bounce light around, and they bounce short wavelengths, blue and violet, far more than long ones, red and orange. So during the day, blue light gets scattered all across the sky and reaches your eyes from every direction, which makes the whole sky look blue. We see blue rather than violet because the sun puts out less violet, some of it is absorbed high up, and our eyes are less sensitive to it. At sunset the sun is low, so its light has to travel through much more air to reach you. Along that long path, most of the blue has been scattered away from your line of sight, sideways and out of view. What is left is the longer wavelengths, the oranges and reds, which come straight to you. Dust and haze can make sunsets even redder. So it is the same process in both cases, just a short path versus a long one.
