ASSISTANT: The Cold War ended because the Soviet system ran out of money, legitimacy and the will to use force, and then it collapsed in a few fast steps between 1989 and 1991. I would pick three events. First, Mikhail Gorbachev's reforms, glasnost and perestroika, which opened debate and loosened control without fixing the economy. Second, the revolutions of 1989 in Eastern Europe, including the fall of the Berlin Wall, when Gorbachev refused to send tanks and the satellite states left Moscow's orbit. Third, the failed hardliner coup in August 1991, which destroyed the Communist Party's authority and made Boris Yeltsin and the republics the real power. The Soviet Union was formally dissolved in December 1991. As for causes versus triggers, the deep causes were a stagnant command economy, the cost of the arms race and the war in Afghanistan, falling oil prices, and rising nationalism in the republics. The immediate triggers were the coup attempt, the republics declaring independence, and the Belovezha agreement in December, where Russia, Ukraine and Belarus declared the union finished.
