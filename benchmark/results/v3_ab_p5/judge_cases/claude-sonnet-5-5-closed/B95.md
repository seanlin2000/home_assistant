# Exchange 1

USER: Imagine I want to build a private, locally hosted voice assistant that can answer general questions, search the web, control smart-home devices, and have natural conversations. I have a $1,000 hardware budget. Propose an architecture, explain the components, identify the biggest limitations, and prioritize what I should build first.

ASSISTANT: With a one thousand dollar budget, I would build around a small mini computer or used mini PC with a lot of memory, rather than a big GPU. For hardware, an Apple Mac mini with sixteen to twenty-four gigabytes of unified memory, or a similar small PC, fits the budget, plus a decent USB microphone and speaker, or a Home Assistant voice preview device. The software would have four parts. First, wake word and speech to text, for example openWakeWord and Whisper. Second, a local language model of about four to eight billion parameters, quantized to four bits, running on llama.cpp or a similar server with tool calling. Third, a text to speech engine such as Piper. Fourth, Home Assistant to control smart-home devices, with the model calling it as a tool, and a search tool for web questions. The biggest limitations are that small models are less accurate and less capable with tools than cloud models, they can make things up, web search sends queries out, and the latency can feel slow. I would build in this order. First, voice in and out with chat. Second, smart-home control. Third, web search. Fourth, refine the personality and memory.
