# Exchange 1

USER: My projector is connected to a JBL Bluetooth speaker. Sometimes the audio becomes choppy when I connect my laptop, and playing video games on the projector causes more eye strain than playing on my monitor. What could cause these problems, and how would you systematically troubleshoot them?

ASSISTANT: There are really two separate problems. For choppy audio, the likely causes are Bluetooth interference and bandwidth. Bluetooth shares the crowded two point four gigahertz band with wifi, so a laptop on wifi near the speaker can cause dropouts. Other causes are distance or obstacles between the laptop and speaker, a weak speaker battery, an outdated Bluetooth driver on the laptop, or the laptop using a low-quality codec or a mode that mixes in microphone audio. Also check whether the speaker is connected to the projector and laptop at once. To troubleshoot, change one thing at a time: move the speaker closer, turn off other Bluetooth devices, switch your laptop wifi to five gigahertz, update drivers, forget the speaker and pair it again, and test with another device. For eye strain, projectors are usually dimmer in contrast and may have lower resolution, so games look softer. Possible causes include a dark room with a bright image, a brightness setting that is too high, a large screen viewed too closely, lower refresh rate and input lag, flicker from a color wheel, and long play sessions. Try ambient lighting, lower brightness, a smaller image, better distance, and breaks.
