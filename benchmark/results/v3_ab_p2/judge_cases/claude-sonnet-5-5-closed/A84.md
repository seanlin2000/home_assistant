Reference sketch (what a correct answer must contain):
A stock is worth the present value of its future cash flows, discounted at a rate that builds on the risk-free rate. When the Fed raises rates the discount rate rises, and cash flows far in the future lose much more present value than near ones (as with long-dated bonds). Growth and technology companies get most of their value from profits expected years ahead, so they are long-duration assets and their valuations fall more. Also: bonds become a more attractive alternative, borrowing costs rise for unprofitable companies, and valuation multiples compress (2022, when the Nasdaq fell about a third, is the standard example). A worked figure helps but is optional: 100 dollars due in 10 years is worth about 74 today at 3 percent and about 56 at 6 percent.

# Exchange 1

USER: Why does the Federal Reserve raising interest rates generally put downward pressure on technology stock valuations? Explain the relationship between interest rates, discounted cash flows, and long-duration growth stocks.

ASSISTANT: A stock is worth the cash it will pay out in the future, discounted back to today's dollars. The discount rate is built from interest rates: safe government bond yields plus a premium for risk. When the Fed raises rates, bond yields usually rise, the discount rate goes up, and every future dollar is worth less today. That hits technology and growth stocks hardest because their cash flows are far in the future. A mature company that earns money now gets much of its value from near-term cash, which is only slightly affected by discounting. A growth company may earn little for years and promise big profits a decade away, and those distant profits shrink a lot when discounted at a higher rate. In bond terms, these stocks have long duration, so they are more sensitive to rate changes. Higher rates also make safe bonds more attractive, raise borrowing costs for companies that rely on cheap funding, and slow spending, all of which add pressure. The effect is strongest when rate increases are unexpected, since markets price expected changes in advance.
