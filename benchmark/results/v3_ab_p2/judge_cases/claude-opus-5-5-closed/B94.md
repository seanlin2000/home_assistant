## Consecutive pass or Swiss Travel Pass Flex?
A consecutive pass runs on successive days. Flex offers 3, 4, 6, 8 or 15 selected travel days within one month. Choose Flex only after marking the days when you need its transport and attraction benefits. Follow the activation instructions supplied with that product before traveling on a selected day.

## How activation works
You activate each travel day before your first ride of that day. Two rules catch people out: a travel day is a calendar day (activate at 6pm and it still ends at midnight), and on non-activated days the pass covers nothing — including museums and mountain discounts. Otherwise it behaves exactly like the regular pass: no seat bookings needed on normal trains, no fare gates, just show the QR code when the conductor comes through. ## How Do Mountain Peak Discounts Differ at Jungfraujoch, Schilthorn, and Zermatt?
### Why Does the Half Fare Card Beat the Swiss Travel Pass on Jungfraujoch?
On Jungfraujoch and Schilthorn, the Swiss Half Fare Card gives a full 50 percent discount. By contrast, the Swiss Travel Pass gives only 25 percent off the expensive upper climb. That single rule flips the break even math for almost every Bernese Oberland trip.
Consider a summer round trip from Interlaken Ost to Jungfraujoch (Top of Europe). In peak summer, the full adult round trip fare costs about CHF 249. With the Swiss Half Fare Card, you pay 50 percent of the total fare, or about CHF 124.50. With the Swiss Travel Pass, your ride from Interlaken Ost up to Grindelwald or Wengen is free. However, the upper mountain leg from Grindelwald or Wengen to Jungfraujoch gets only a 25 percent discount. As a result, a Swiss Travel Pass holder still pays about CHF 160 out of pocket for Jungfraujoch.
