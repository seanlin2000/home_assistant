Choppy audio: the speaker may also be paired with the laptop, so the laptop competes for the connection (multipoint); 2.4 GHz interference from Wi-Fi and the laptop's own Bluetooth; distance or obstacles; a weak Bluetooth chip or codec in the projector. # Exchange 1

USER: My projector is connected to a JBL Bluetooth speaker. Sometimes the audio becomes choppy when I connect my laptop, and playing video games on the projector causes more eye strain than playing on my monitor. What could cause these problems, and how would you systematically troubleshoot them?

ASSISTANT: These are likely two separate problems. Choppy audio usually means Bluetooth trouble: the speaker paired to both the projector and the laptop so they fight over it, interference from two point four gigahertz Wi-Fi, distance or obstacles, or a weak Bluetooth chip in the projector. Eye strain from projector gaming often comes from a very bright image in a dark room, a low refresh rate or lag, the rainbow effect on single-chip DLP projectors, soft focus or heavy keystone correction, or sitting too close to a huge image. Troubleshoot one change at a time. For audio, unpair the speaker from everything except the projector, then test with the laptop's Bluetooth off and its Wi-Fi on five gigahertz, move the speaker closer, and try a cable from the projector's audio out; if the cable sounds clean, Bluetooth is the culprit. For your eyes, add some room light, lower the brightness, turn on game mode, check the refresh rate, refocus, fix the placement instead of using keystone, and sit farther back, then compare an hour on each setup.
