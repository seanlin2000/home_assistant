ASSISTANT: Before taxes, your stake would be worth about one hundred sixty-six thousand, seven hundred dollars, roughly an eight point three times multiple, for a profit of about one hundred forty-six thousand, seven hundred dollars. Here's how it works. Twenty thousand dollars at a sixty million dollar post-money valuation buys you one three-thousandth of the company, about zero point zero three three percent. Fifty percent cumulative dilution halves that to about zero point zero one seven percent. That share of a one billion dollar sale comes to about one hundred sixty-six thousand, seven hundred dollars. This assumes you hold the same kind of shares as everyone else and the sale proceeds are split evenly. In reality, later investors usually have liquidation preferences that are paid first, and proceeds can be reduced by debt, fees and employee payouts, so your actual check could be somewhat lower. On the other hand, if you had pro rata rights and invested in later rounds, you could have limited the dilution.
