Reference sketch (what a correct answer must contain):
The stock market reflects expected profits of large, often multinational companies, and stock ownership is concentrated (in the US the richest tenth of households own the large majority of stocks), while people feel the economy through wages, prices, rent and jobs. Three concrete examples from any of: prices rising faster than wages while companies keep or raise margins; a handful of giant tech firms driving index gains while most businesses and regions lag; companies earning much of their profit abroad; cost cutting and layoffs that raise profits and share prices; low rates or stimulus inflating asset prices, which helps people who own assets (the K-shaped recovery of 2020 and 2021); housing costs climbing while the index climbs. Must give three distinct, concrete mechanisms, not three restatements of "the market is not the economy".

# Exchange 1

USER: Why can a country have a strong stock market while its average citizens feel like the economy is getting worse? Give three concrete examples of how this happens.

ASSISTANT: A stock market measures the profits and expectations of large listed companies, not the finances of ordinary households, so the two can drift apart. Here are three ways it happens. First, ownership is concentrated. A small share of households own most stocks, so when the market rises, the gains go mostly to the wealthy while everyone else sees nothing. Second, prices can outrun wages. Companies can post record profits through automation, layoffs, or overseas production, and meanwhile rent, groceries and healthcare get more expensive faster than paychecks grow, so people feel poorer even as share prices climb. Third, markets look forward and can be lifted by factors that do not touch daily life, such as a few giant tech firms dominating the index, big buybacks, or expectations of lower interest rates, while ordinary people face high borrowing costs, job insecurity, or weak hiring. The index can also be dragged up by a handful of companies while most of the economy does not benefit. So a strong market tells you about capital owners, not about how a typical family feels.
