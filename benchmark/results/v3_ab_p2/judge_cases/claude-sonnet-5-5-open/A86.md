Reference sketch (what a correct answer must contain):
The Hue Bridge is the local hub: it talks to the bulbs over Zigbee and keeps scenes and automations. Share control by inviting the other people in the Hue app or letting them use it on the home network, add physical Hue switches for anyone without a phone, and connect a voice assistant (Apple Home through the bridge, Alexa, Google Home, or a local controller such as Home Assistant). If the internet goes down: the bridge, its automations, the Hue app on the local network and physical switches keep working; Apple Home with a home hub keeps working locally; cloud voice assistants such as Alexa and Google stop taking commands, and control from outside the home stops. A local voice setup avoids that dependency.

# Exchange 1

USER: I have Philips Hue lights connected to a Hue Bridge. I want multiple people in my apartment to control the lights, including through voice commands. What is the best architecture for this, and what happens if the internet goes down?

ASSISTANT: The best setup is to keep the Hue Bridge as the hub and add a home hub on top of it. Put the bridge on your wired network, give everyone access through the Hue app on their own phones by inviting them to your home, and connect it to a voice assistant everyone already uses, such as Alexa, Google Home or Apple Home, so anyone in the room can speak commands. Add a few physical Hue dimmer switches or smart buttons so guests do not need an app at all. If you want local automation, Home Assistant can connect to the bridge over your local network. As for the internet going down, the bridge keeps working locally. Lights still respond to the physical switches, to the Hue app on your home Wi-Fi, and to automations stored on the bridge. What stops are cloud-dependent things, mainly voice commands that are processed online, and remote control from outside the apartment. So for the most resilient setup, rely on switches and local control, and treat voice as a convenience that will pause during an outage.
