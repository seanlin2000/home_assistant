Reference sketch (what a correct answer must contain):
The Hue Bridge is the local hub: it talks to the bulbs over Zigbee and keeps scenes and automations. Share control by inviting the other people in the Hue app or letting them use it on the home network, add physical Hue switches for anyone without a phone, and connect a voice assistant (Apple Home through the bridge, Alexa, Google Home, or a local controller such as Home Assistant). If the internet goes down: the bridge, its automations, the Hue app on the local network and physical switches keep working; Apple Home with a home hub keeps working locally; cloud voice assistants such as Alexa and Google stop taking commands, and control from outside the home stops. A local voice setup avoids that dependency.

# Exchange 1

USER: I have Philips Hue lights connected to a Hue Bridge. I want multiple people in my apartment to control the lights, including through voice commands. What is the best architecture for this, and what happens if the internet goes down?

ASSISTANT: The best setup is to keep the Hue Bridge as the hub that talks to the lights over Zigbee, then connect it to a local smart-home controller such as Home Assistant, which gives each person access and handles voice. Set up the Hue app for each person, with their own accounts or home invitations, so everyone can control lights from their phone. For voice, use either a cloud assistant like Alexa, Google, or Siri, which is easiest, or a local voice setup through Home Assistant, which keeps commands private and can work without internet. Add physical Hue dimmer switches or smart buttons so guests can control lights without any app or voice. If the internet goes down, here is what happens. The lights still work with physical switches, and the bridge still controls them over your local network. The Hue app on the same Wi-Fi usually still works locally. Home Assistant automations and local voice also keep working. What stops working is cloud voice assistants, remote access when you're away, and anything that relies on a cloud service. Routines stored on the bridge itself, such as schedules, generally continue.
