Choppy audio: the speaker may also be paired with the laptop, so the laptop competes for the connection (multipoint); 2.4 GHz interference from Wi-Fi and the laptop's own Bluetooth; distance or obstacles; a weak Bluetooth chip or codec in the projector. # Exchange 1

USER: My projector is connected to a JBL Bluetooth speaker. Sometimes the audio becomes choppy when I connect my laptop, and playing video games on the projector causes more eye strain than playing on my monitor. What could cause these problems, and how would you systematically troubleshoot them?

ASSISTANT: These are two separate problems, so tackle them one at a time. Choppy Bluetooth audio usually comes from radio interference or a weak link. Bluetooth shares the two point four gigahertz band with Wi-Fi, and a laptop streaming video over Wi-Fi nearby, or connecting to the projector wirelessly, can crowd it out. The projector's Bluetooth chip may also struggle when it's processing more video. To troubleshoot, move the speaker closer with a clear line of sight, switch your Wi-Fi to five gigahertz, try a different video source to see whether it's only the laptop, update the projector's firmware, and if possible use a wired audio cable, which also removes Bluetooth's lag in games. For eye strain, the likely causes are input lag and motion smoothing, a soft image from imperfect focus or keystone correction, too much brightness in a dark room or too little in a bright one, the rainbow flicker some DLP projectors show, and sitting so close that your eyes sweep a huge image. Turn on game mode, turn off motion smoothing, fix focus, add soft room light, and sit farther back.
