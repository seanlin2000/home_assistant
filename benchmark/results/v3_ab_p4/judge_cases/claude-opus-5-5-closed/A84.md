Reference sketch (what a correct answer must contain):
A stock is worth the present value of its future cash flows, discounted at a rate that builds on the risk-free rate. When the Fed raises rates the discount rate rises, and cash flows far in the future lose much more present value than near ones (as with long-dated bonds). Growth and technology companies get most of their value from profits expected years ahead, so they are long-duration assets and their valuations fall more. Also: bonds become a more attractive alternative, borrowing costs rise for unprofitable companies, and valuation multiples compress (2022, when the Nasdaq fell about a third, is the standard example). A worked figure helps but is optional: 100 dollars due in 10 years is worth about 74 today at 3 percent and about 56 at 6 percent.

# Exchange 1

USER: Why does the Federal Reserve raising interest rates generally put downward pressure on technology stock valuations? Explain the relationship between interest rates, discounted cash flows, and long-duration growth stocks.

ASSISTANT: Because a stock is worth the cash it will produce in the future, translated into today's dollars, and higher interest rates make future dollars worth less today. Investors value a company with what's called a discounted cash flow: they estimate its future profits and shrink each year's amount by a discount rate, which starts from the risk-free interest rate plus a premium for risk. When the Fed raises rates, that discount rate rises, and every future dollar gets shrunk more. The effect compounds with time. A dollar expected next year barely changes, but a dollar expected fifteen years from now loses a lot of its present value. Mature companies like utilities earn most of their cash soon, so they're less affected. Fast-growing tech companies often expect most of their profits far in the future, which makes them long-duration assets, a bit like long-term bonds, and so their valuations fall harder. Higher rates also raise borrowing costs for growth companies, and make safe bonds a more attractive alternative, pulling money out of riskier stocks.
