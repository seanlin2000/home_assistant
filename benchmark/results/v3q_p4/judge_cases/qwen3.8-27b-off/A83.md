# Exchange 1

USER: I invest $20,000 in a startup at a $60 million post-money valuation. The company subsequently raises several funding rounds, resulting in 50% cumulative dilution. If the company eventually sells for $1 billion, what is my investment worth before taxes? Calculate my multiple and profit.

ASSISTANT: Your ownership works out like this: you put in $20,000 at a $60 million post-money valuation, which gives you 20,000 divided by 60,000,000, or about 0.0333 percent of the company.

After 50% cumulative dilution, your stake is cut in half to roughly 0.0167 percent. At a $1 billion exit, that slice is worth about $166,667 before taxes.

So:
- Multiple on investment: about 8.3x (you get back roughly eight and a third times what you put in)
- Profit: about $146,667 ($166,667 minus your original $20,000)

A couple of caveats worth keeping in mind: I assumed the dilution is applied uniformly to all existing shareholders and that you have no liquidation preference or anti-dilution protection, which in a real deal could change the math. I also assumed the $1 billion is an enterprise value split pro rata across shares; if it's equity value with debt on the books, your number would be a bit lower.
