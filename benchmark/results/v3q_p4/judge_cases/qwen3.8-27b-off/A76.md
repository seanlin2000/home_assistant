USER: How did the Cold War end? Explain the three most important events that led to the dissolution of the Soviet Union, and distinguish between the causes and the immediate triggers.

ASSISTANT: The short version is this: the Cold War ended because the Soviet system was already sick — economically exhausted, politically brittle, and losing its own people's belief in it — and then a string of events in 1989 through 1991 turned that sickness into an actual collapse. The three most important events are Gorbachev's reforms starting around 1985, the fall of the Berlin Wall in November 1989, and the failed August coup in Moscow in 1991.

The first is the real cause, not a trigger: perestroika and glasnost. By the mid-1980s the Soviet economy was stalling — heavy industry consuming everything, consumer goods scarce, oil revenues falling, and the arms race with the United States draining money it didn't have. Gorbachev tried to fix this from inside the system with economic restructuring and political openness. What he could not foresee is that once you let people speak freely and hold elections, they immediately demand what the system cannot give: real freedom and prosperity. So the cause was decades of structural decay, and Gorbachev's reforms were the thing that finally made that decay visible and unstoppable.

The second event is where causes become triggers: the fall of the Berlin Wall on November 9, 1989. That was a moment when the Soviet grip on Eastern Europe simply snapped. Poland, Hungary, Czechoslovakia followed within months, all without war, because Gorbachev refused to send tanks — a decisive break from the Brezhnev Doctrine. The wall's fall wasn't what weakened the USSR; it was the visible proof that the whole imperial structure was already gone, and it accelerated everything after it.

The third is the immediate trigger for the actual dissolution: the August 1991 coup. Hardline communists tried to overthrow Gorbachev, and it failed in about three days — but that failure destroyed whatever authority the central government still had. Russia's leader Yeltsin rode out the coup as a hero, the Soviet Union as an institution was finished in everyone's eyes, and within months the Baltic states, Ukraine, and others seceded. The USSR was formally dissolved on December 25, 1991, when Gorbachev resigned and handed over the nuclear codes to Yeltsin.

So to draw the line you asked about: the causes were long-term — economic stagnation, the cost of the arms race, ethnic tensions inside the union, and a legitimacy crisis that Glasnost exposed. The triggers were short-term — the wall's fall in late 1989 showing Eastern Europe was lost, and the failed coup in summer 1991 destroying the last shred of central power. One prepared the ground for years; the other two happened within eighteen months and did the rest.
