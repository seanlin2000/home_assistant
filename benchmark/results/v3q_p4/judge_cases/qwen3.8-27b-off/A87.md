USER: My projector is connected to a JBL Bluetooth speaker. Sometimes the audio becomes choppy when I connect my laptop, and playing video games on the projector causes more eye strain than playing on my monitor. What could cause these problems, and how would you systematically troubleshoot them?

ASSISTANT: Both of those are very fixable, and they're probably unrelated to each other. Let me take them in turn.

The choppy audio is almost certainly a bandwidth or interference problem on the Bluetooth link. A couple of things can cause it. First, your laptop, once connected, may also be trying to use the same speaker as its audio output, so the projector and the laptop are fighting over one low-bandwidth connection. Second, if your laptop is using 2.4 gigahertz Wi-Fi or a 2.4 gigahertz USB wireless dongle, it can crowd the same radio band that Bluetooth uses, especially in an apartment where neighboring Wi-Fi networks are everywhere. Third, plain distance, obstacles like a TV or metal cabinet between the devices, or just a weak speaker battery will all cause dropouts.

To troubleshoot it systematically, I'd isolate variables one at a time. Start by unplugging the laptop entirely and see if the projector-to-speaker audio is smooth on its own. If it is, reconnect the laptop but open its sound settings and make sure the JBL speaker is not selected as the laptop's output device; you want only the projector sending audio to it. Then check the distance and line of sight between the projector and the speaker, move them closer if you can, and turn off anything on 2.4 gigahertz Wi-Fi near them to see if the choppiness goes away. If your laptop's Wi-Fi has a five gigahertz band available, moving it there often clears up Bluetooth interference immediately. Finally, make sure both devices' firmware is current and that the speaker isn't running on a nearly dead battery.

The eye strain while gaming on the projector is a different beast, and it comes down to image quality rather than sound. Projectors are typically dimmer than a monitor, especially in a room with any ambient light, so your eyes have to work harder to see contrast. They also tend to have a lower effective resolution or softer edges, particularly if you're projecting onto a wall instead of a proper screen, and the image may be slightly out of focus or misaligned at certain parts of the frame. Motion blur is usually worse on a projector too, because many projectors run at sixty hertz with less motion processing than a good monitor, which makes fast game action harder to track and tires your eyes faster.
