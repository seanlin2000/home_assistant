## Open Source vs Open Weight, in One Minute
Almost every model on this list is open weight, not open source. The distinction matters more than the marketing suggests.
Open weight means you can download the model parameters, run them on your own hardware, and usually use them commercially. It does not mean you can see the training data or reproduce the model. The Open Source Initiative's Open Source AI Definition requires three things: detailed data information, the complete training code, and the parameters, all under open terms. Weights alone do not clear that bar.
By the strict OSI reading, GLM-5.3, DeepSeek V4, and Llama 4 are all open weight, and so is this ranking's newest permissive entry, Xiaomi's MIT-licensed MiMo-V2.6-Pro. The models that genuinely qualify as open source, like AI2's OLMo or EleutherAI's Pythia, publish training data and recipes but do not top capability leaderboards. NVIDIA's Nemotron 3 comes closest to bridging the two: it ships weights alongside training datasets and recipes, the closest any frontier-adjacent lab comes to the OSI bar, even though its mid-pack scores keep it out of the ranked eleven below (see Near Misses).
