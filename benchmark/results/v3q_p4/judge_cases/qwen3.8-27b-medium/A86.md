Reference sketch (what a correct answer must contain):
The Hue Bridge is the local hub: it talks to the bulbs over Zigbee and keeps scenes and automations. Share control by inviting the other people in the Hue app or letting them use it on the home network, add physical Hue switches for anyone without a phone, and connect a voice assistant (Apple Home through the bridge, Alexa, Google Home, or a local controller such as Home Assistant). If the internet goes down: the bridge, its automations, the Hue app on the local network and physical switches keep working; Apple Home with a home hub keeps working locally; cloud voice assistants such as Alexa and Google stop taking commands, and control from outside the home stops. A local voice setup avoids that dependency.

ASSISTANT: Here's the setup I'd recommend, and it's actually pretty simple once you think of it in layers.

The foundation is one Hue Bridge, connected to your router by Ethernet, with all the lights paired to it. Then one person creates the main Hue account, and in the Hue app under Settings, Home access, you invite each other person in the apartment. Everyone accepts the invite with their own Hue account, so nobody is sharing a password, and each of you can control the same rooms and scenes from your own phone, with whatever permissions the owner gives them. That handles the multi-person app side cleanly.

For voice, you pick one assistant ecosystem and connect the same Hue home to it once. With Alexa, you enable the Philips Hue skill, sign into the Hue account, and link the bridge; then anyone else in the apartment can join the same Alexa household on their own devices and use the lights. Google Home and Apple Home work the same idea. The practical tip here is to name your rooms and scenes clearly and consistently — "living room," "movie scene," that kind of thing — so voice commands work predictably for everyone instead of each person learning a different vocabulary.

Now the internet question. Hue has two control paths. The first is local: your bulbs talk to the bridge over Zigbee, and the bridge lives on your LAN. As long as your router and Wi-Fi are up, the Hue app on a phone still controls everything instantly, even with the internet completely down, and Hue's physical wall switches and sensors keep working too, since the bridge runs those automations locally. What breaks without internet is the cloud path: remote access from outside the home, and voice assistants, because Alexa, Google, and Apple route their Hue commands through the cloud to reach the bridge. So in an outage, "Alexa, lights off" would stop working, but the app and wall switches would not.

If offline voice control matters a lot to you, the more advanced option is a home server running Home Assistant with a local voice assistant, which talks to the bridge over the local network with no cloud in the loop. That's a real project, though — more setup, more maintenance, and a learning curve — so for most apartments the standard bridge-plus-assistant setup with the understanding that voice needs the internet is the right trade-off.
