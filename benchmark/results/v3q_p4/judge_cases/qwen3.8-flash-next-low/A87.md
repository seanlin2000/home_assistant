ASSISTANT: Both problems are almost certainly fixable, and they have quite different root causes. Let me take them in order.

The choppy audio is a radio problem in nearly every case like this. Bluetooth lives in the 2.4 GHz band, the same band most laptops use for Wi-Fi, and in a laptop the two radios usually share one chip and sometimes one antenna, so heavy Wi-Fi traffic can crowd Bluetooth out. Microsoft's own support forums describe exactly your symptom, and the reported fix is to move the laptop onto a 5 GHz network. Two other culprits are worth checking: USB 3.0 ports, hubs, external drives and unshielded USB 3.0 cables radiate noise right across 2.4 GHz, and Windows power management can power down the Bluetooth adapter when it thinks the device is idle, which shows up as dropouts during quiet passages. There is also a sneaky audio-side cause, which is the profile rather than the signal. If the laptop ever negotiates the Hands-Free profile instead of the stereo A2DP profile, the audio drops to telephone quality and sounds broken and stuttery, and that happens whenever something grabs the microphone, such as a game's voice chat or a video call app. Finally, if you connect the laptop to the projector wirelessly, by AirPlay or Miracast, then the projector itself is running Wi-Fi and Bluetooth at the same time, which is the worst possible combination for a Bluetooth speaker link.

For that one I'd bisect rather than change several things at once. Start by playing music from your phone through the JBL in the same spot while nothing else is transmitting. If that is clean, the speaker is fine and the problem is upstream in the chain. Then turn the laptop's Wi-Fi off entirely, or run it on 5 GHz or Ethernet, and see whether the stuttering vanishes; if it does, interference is confirmed. Next unplug any USB 3.0 drives, hubs and dongles near the projector and speaker, and if you use a wireless adapter or dongle move it to a USB 2.0 port or put it on a short extension cable so it sits away from the machine. Then disable power saving, specifically USB selective suspend and the "allow the computer to turn off this device" setting on the Bluetooth adapter in Device Manager, and make sure the Bluetooth and Wi-Fi drivers are current. After that, check the audio profile and codec, disabling the hands-free telephony service and, if your JBL supports it in the JBL app, forcing a more robust codec instead of the highest-bitrate one, since codecs like LDAC trade stability for quality and fall apart under interference. Move the speaker closer to the projector with a clear line of sight, since a body or a wall between them matters more than you'd expect, and update the speaker's firmware. If it still stutters, the practical escape is wired, either a 3.5 mm or optical cable from the projector, or a USB Bluetooth 5 adapter on the laptop so the Bluetooth link is physically separated from the internal combo radio.
