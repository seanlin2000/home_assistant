ASSISTANT: Your twenty thousand dollars buys you one thirty‑three thousandths of a percent of the company — twenty thousand out of sixty million, which is 0.0333 percent. After the subsequent rounds cut that in half, you own 0.0167 percent at the time of sale.

Applied to a one billion dollar exit, that stake is worth about $166,700 before taxes. That's a return of roughly 8.3 times your money, for a profit of about $146,700.

Two caveats worth keeping in mind. First, this assumes your stake gets diluted proportionally with everyone else and that there's no liquidation preference stacked ahead of you — if the later rounds come with preferred shares that get paid out first, common holders like early angels can end up with noticeably less. Second, I'm reading "50% cumulative dilution" as your ownership being halved across all the rounds combined; if you meant something else, like the company issued enough new shares that its total count doubled and then kept going, the math would change.
