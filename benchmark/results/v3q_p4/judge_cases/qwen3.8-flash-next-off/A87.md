**The choppy audio**

Bluetooth audio lives in the 2.4 GHz band, which is exactly where a lot of Wi-Fi and other wireless gear also operates. So the usual suspects are radio interference, the laptop's Bluetooth driver, Windows power management putting the adapter to sleep, the speaker being registered as two devices (stereo versus hands-free headset mode), and simple range or obstruction issues. Guidance from Microsoft's own support pages and several driver-focused sites all converge on the same short list of causes.

The fact that it happens specifically when you connect your laptop is a useful clue: it points at the laptop, not the projector or the speaker. A clean way to isolate it:

1. Turn the laptop's Wi-Fi off entirely and play something over Bluetooth. If the stuttering stops, interference is your answer, and the fix is moving the laptop onto a 5 GHz or 6 GHz Wi-Fi band so the 2.4 GHz spectrum is freer for Bluetooth.
2. Check whether the JBL shows up twice in the sound output list, as a speaker and as a headset. Hands-free mode uses a much lower-quality codec and is a common source of crackle, especially when something requests the microphone, like voice chat in a game.
3. Move the speaker within a few feet of the laptop with nothing solid between them, and disconnect any other Bluetooth devices you aren't using. If proximity fixes it, range or obstruction was the cause.
4. Install the Bluetooth driver from your laptop manufacturer's support page for your exact model rather than the generic one Windows supplies. Multiple sources report this alone fixing stutter that the Windows driver caused. Also check Device Manager's power management tab and stop Windows turning the adapter off.
5. Look at physical layout: USB 3.0 ports emit interference right in the 2.4 GHz range, so if you have a USB Bluetooth dongle or anything USB 3 near the laptop, move it to a USB 2 port or put it on a short extension cable away from the body of the machine. Keep it away from the router and the microwave too.
