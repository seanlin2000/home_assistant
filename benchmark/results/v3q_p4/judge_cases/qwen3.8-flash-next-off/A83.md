ASSISTANT: Your twenty thousand dollars buys about 0.0333 percent of the company at a sixty million post-money valuation, or roughly one share in three thousand. After fifty percent cumulative dilution, that stake shrinks to about 0.0167 percent. Applied to a one billion dollar sale, your position is worth approximately $166,700 — call it $166,667 before taxes.

That's a profit of about $146,700, and a return multiple of 8.33 times your money, which works out to an 833 percent gain.

Two caveats worth keeping in mind. First, "50% cumulative dilution" is doing a lot of work here: it assumes every later round cuts your percentage roughly in half overall, with no anti-dilution protection, and it ignores the option pool set aside for employees at your round, which usually dilutes you a bit on day one. Second, this is all pre-tax and pre-liquidation-preference math. If the later rounds came with preferred stock carrying liquidation preferences, common-style holders like you could end up with materially less than this simple percentage-of-the-sale figure.
