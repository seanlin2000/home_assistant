The Fed did not cut rates to zero last month. At its October 28 meeting the FOMC held the federal funds target range at 3.75 to 4 percent, unchanged from a quarter-point *increase* it made in September. The vote was 10 to 2, and notably both dissenters wanted to go the other way, pushing for another 25-basis-point hike rather than a cut. So rates are not near zero, and the direction of travel in recent meetings has been up, not down.

Mortgage rates reflect that. As of this week, the average 30-year fixed refinance rate is sitting around 7.1 percent, with different trackers landing between roughly 6.9 and 7.2 percent depending on how they weight lenders. The 15-year refi average is around 6.2 percent. Those numbers have been drifting up slightly week to week rather than falling.

So the honest answer is: nothing about the Fed's recent moves makes refinancing attractive right now. Whether it's worth it for *you* depends almost entirely on the rate you're currently paying, and I don't know that yet. The rough rule of thumb is that refinancing at today's roughly 7 percent only makes sense if your existing rate is meaningfully higher than that, say mid-2020 through early-2022 vintage paper in the 3 to 4 percent range, or a more recent loan that got done above 7.5 percent. If you're already at 6 percent or below, closing costs of maybe 2 to 3 percent of the loan balance would take years of monthly savings to recoup, and you'd likely be better off putting that money toward principal instead.

Tell me your current rate, your remaining balance, and roughly how many years are left on the loan, and I'll work out the actual monthly savings and how many months it would take to break even on closing costs.
