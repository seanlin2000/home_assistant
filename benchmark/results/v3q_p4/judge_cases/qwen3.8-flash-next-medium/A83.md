ASSISTANT: Here's how it works out.

At a 60 million dollar post-money valuation, your 20,000 dollars buys roughly 0.033 percent of the company, or about three and one third hundredths of a percent. After 50 percent cumulative dilution from the later rounds, that share is cut in half to about 0.0167 percent.

On a one billion dollar sale, that stake is worth about **166,700 dollars** before taxes.

The multiple is 8.3 times your money, which you can also see the quick way: the company grew 16.7 times in value from 60 million to a billion, and dilution handed back half of that. Your profit, before taxes, is roughly **146,700 dollars**.

Two caveats worth flagging. A 50 percent cumulative dilution figure usually counts only new shares issued in later rounds, which is what I assumed here, so it ignores any option pool expansion or secondary sales. And the tax side can matter a lot: if you held the shares long enough, some or all of that gain may qualify for long-term capital gains rates, and if the stock was qualified small business stock held over five years, Section 1202 could exclude a meaningful chunk of the gain entirely. That's worth a conversation with a tax advisor before you spend the 146,700 dollars.
