**The choppy audio**

The most likely culprit is radio congestion. Bluetooth and older Wi‑Fi both live in the 2.4 GHz band, and Bluetooth hops across that whole band up to about 1,600 times a second while your Wi‑Fi sits on a channel in it. When your laptop connects and starts pushing data, it crowds the air that your projector and JBL speaker are using, and the audio packets arrive late. A Windows discussion thread on exactly this symptom says the fix was simply moving to a 5 GHz Wi‑Fi network.

So the first test is cheap and decisive: turn the laptop's Wi‑Fi off completely, or move it to a 5 GHz or 6 GHz band, and play something. If the stutter stops, interference is your answer. On Windows you can confirm which band you're actually on with the command netsh wlan show interfaces in a terminal; channels 1 through 13 mean 2.4 GHz. Don't trust that your router "supports 5 GHz" — the laptop may still be connecting on 2.4.

Other likely causes, in the order I'd check them:

- USB 3.0 noise. Unshielded USB 3 ports and cables radiate broadband noise right into the 2.4 GHz range. Intel has published on this. If you have an external drive, dock, or USB Bluetooth dongle plugged in next to the port the laptop uses, move things apart, use a USB 2 port, or put the dongle on a short shielded extension cable.
- Power saving. Windows can power down the Bluetooth adapter when it thinks the machine is idle, which shows up as dropouts during quiet passages. In Device Manager, on the Bluetooth adapter's properties, untick "Allow the computer to turn off this device to save power."
- Codec bitrate. If the chain is using a high‑bandwidth codec like LDAC at 990 kbps, it can simply be too much for a crowded band. Switching to AAC or SBC often makes audio rock solid at the cost of some fidelity.
- Distance, obstacles and body shadowing. Bluetooth does poorly through walls, metal desks and people. If the laptop is on the far side of the room from the speaker, moving it to line of sight can matter more than any setting.
- Drivers. Skip the generic driver Windows installs and get the Bluetooth driver for your exact laptop model from the manufacturer's support page. Rolling back a recent driver update is also worth trying if this started after one.
- The speaker itself. Test the JBL with a phone across the room. If the phone is fine and the laptop chain is not, the problem is in your PC or projector link, not the speaker. Many JBL speakers also support multipoint or paired‑with‑a‑second‑speaker modes, and a half‑connected second device will cause stutter, so check the JBL's app for anything extra it's trying to hold onto.

The discipline that makes this fast is logging each test: what you changed, what you heard. One good pass isn't proof, because a lull in your network traffic can mimic a fix.

- Refresh rate. Most projector game setups run 60 Hz; if your monitor is 120, 144 or higher, the projector is throwing motion far less smoothly and your eye is working harder to track targets.
