Reference sketch (what a correct answer must contain):
The Hue Bridge is the local hub: it talks to the bulbs over Zigbee and keeps scenes and automations. Share control by inviting the other people in the Hue app or letting them use it on the home network, add physical Hue switches for anyone without a phone, and connect a voice assistant (Apple Home through the bridge, Alexa, Google Home, or a local controller such as Home Assistant). If the internet goes down: the bridge, its automations, the Hue app on the local network and physical switches keep working; Apple Home with a home hub keeps working locally; cloud voice assistants such as Alexa and Google stop taking commands, and control from outside the home stops. A local voice setup avoids that dependency.

# Exchange 1

USER: I have Philips Hue lights connected to a Hue Bridge. I want multiple people in my apartment to control the lights, including through voice commands. What is the best architecture for this, and what happens if the internet goes down?

ASSISTANT: The cleanest setup is to make the bridge the single source of truth and give everyone a local path to it.

For each person, install the Hue app and have them sign in with their own Hue account, then join your bridge as a user — the bridge supports up to 25 users, so a few housemates are easy. That gives each of them direct app and room-level control on the local network.

For voice, pair the same bridge with a voice assistant per person — Alexa or Google Home for each, or Apple HomeKit (via the Hue app) if you want a local option that doesn't lean on their cloud.

If the internet drops: the lights stay on your local Zigbee network through the bridge on Wi-Fi, so the Hue app still works offline, and HomeKit plus Alexa/Google local-control commands still turn things on and off. What you lose is anything needing the cloud — remote access, Hue Sync, and creating new automations.

One caveat worth flagging: exact offline behavior for a given assistant depends on firmware and their local-control support, which does shift over time.
