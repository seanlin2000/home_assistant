# Turn 1

USER: I live in a 500-square-foot studio and want a voice assistant that can hear me from across the room. I care more about microphone quality and natural conversation than smart-home automation. Give me the main hardware design considerations without recommending specific products.

ASSISTANT: The single biggest factor is the microphone array. You want several microphones spaced apart with beamforming, so the device can focus on your voice from across the room, plus acoustic echo cancellation so it can hear you over its own speaker. Second is placement, out in the open, away from walls, the fridge, and the air conditioner, roughly at head height when you are seated. Third is where the wake word runs. It should be detected on the device itself so the microphones are not streaming audio anywhere until you speak the wake word. Fourth is the round trip. Speech recognition, the language model, and speech synthesis should all run on a machine on your local network with enough memory to keep them loaded, because every hop adds delay and natural conversation dies above a couple of seconds. Finally, keep the speaker physically separate from the microphones if you can, and give the whole thing a wired or strong wireless connection, since dropouts feel like the assistant ignoring you.
