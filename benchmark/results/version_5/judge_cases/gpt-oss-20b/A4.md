USER: My plant is growing new leaves quickly near the top, but its older lower leaves are gradually yellowing and dying. The plant is otherwise healthy. What are the most likely explanations, and what information would you want before deciding whether this is normal or a problem?

ASSISTANT: It sounds like your plant is putting out new growth at the tip while the older leaves at the base are turning yellow and falling off. That’s actually a fairly common pattern in many houseplants. The most likely explanations are a natural aging cycle where the plant reallocates nutrients from older leaves to fuel new growth, a mild nutrient deficiency (especially nitrogen or iron), or a slight over‑watering or under‑watering issue that stresses the lower leaves first. Sometimes it can also be a sign of light limitation—if the plant is getting too much light at the top, the lower leaves may bleach out.

Before deciding if this is normal or a problem, it would help to know a few details: how long the plant has been in its current spot, the light intensity and duration it receives, its watering routine (how often and how much water), and whether you’ve fertilized it recently or changed its potting mix. Also, check the soil moisture—if it’s consistently soggy or completely dry, that could point to watering issues. With that information, you can tell if the yellowing is just a normal senescence of older leaves or if you need to adjust light, water, or nutrition.
