USER: Explain why VRAM capacity can matter more than raw GPU speed when choosing hardware for a local LLM. Answer in a way that would sound natural if spoken aloud in under 45 seconds.

ASSISTANT: When you run a language model locally, the biggest bottleneck isn’t how fast the GPU can clock; it’s whether the model’s tensors fit in the GPU’s memory. Even a very fast GPU will stall if it has to swap data back and forth between RAM and the card, which is slow and throws away precious compute cycles. A model with a few hundred megabytes of weights can run quickly on a modest GPU, but a larger model that needs several gigabytes of VRAM will either be impossible to load or will suffer from constant paging, making it slower overall. So, for local LLMs, having enough VRAM to hold the entire model and its activations is usually more important than raw clock speed.
