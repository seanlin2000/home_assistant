ASSISTANT: With approximately 16 gigabytes of GPU memory, you are in a very strong position to run several high-performing models from the Qwen family. The best choices for your hardware depend on whether you prioritize "dense" models (which are generally easier to optimize) or "MoE" (Mixture of Experts) models, which offer high intelligence with faster inference speeds.

The Qwen 3.6 and 3.5 series are the current standards for local use. For your specific hardware, the Qwen 3.6-27B model is a top-tier choice; it is a dense model that, when quantized to a 4-bit level, fits comfortably within 16GB of VRAM while providing performance that rivals very large models like GPT-4o. If you prefer a "thinking" model for complex logic, the QwQ-32B model is excellent, though you must be mindful of its long reasoning chains which can consume a significant portion of your available context window.

When choosing between these, you have to balance three main factors:

First, model size versus quality. A 32B model like the Qwen 3.6-32B or the QwQ-32B will provide significantly more "intelligence" and nuanced reasoning than the 7B or 14B versions, but it leaves less room for a long conversation history (context) in your memory.

Second, quantization is your primary tool for staying under the 16GB limit. Using a 4-bit quantization (like Q4_K_M) is the standard "sweet spot." It drastically reduces the memory footprint of a model—for example, allowing a 30B or 32B model to fit on a single consumer GPU—while only resulting in a negligible loss in perceived intelligence.

Third, speed versus architecture. The Qwen 3.6-30B-A3B is a Mixture of Experts model. Because it only activates a small fraction of its total parameters during each step of generation, it can be much faster than a dense model of a similar total size, making it a great choice if you want high-quality responses without waiting as long for the text to appear.
