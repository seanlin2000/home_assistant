ASSISTANT: The recent price increases for NVIDIA GPUs are driven by a structural shift in the market where AI development is cannibalizing the supply and capacity meant for consumer gaming. 

There are three main drivers behind this:

First, there is a massive shortage of high-bandwidth memory, known as HBM. Memory manufacturers like SK Hynix and Samsung are prioritizing the production of HBM for expensive data-center accelerators, which means less capacity is being allocated to the GDDR memory used in consumer cards.

Second, NVIDIA has been redirecting its own resources toward more profitable products. Reports indicate they have cut production of consumer-focused Blackwell chips, like the RTX 50-series, by as much as thirty to forty percent to ensure they can meet the overwhelming demand for high-margin AI hardware.

Finally, there is a major bottleneck in advanced packaging. The specialized process used to assemble these chips, called CoWoS, is currently unable to keep up with demand. Because every wafer used for an AI chip is a wafer that can't be used for a gaming chip, the competition for factory space has driven up the baseline cost for everyone.
