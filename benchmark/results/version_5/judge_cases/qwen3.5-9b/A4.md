USER: My plant is growing new leaves quickly near the top, but its older lower leaves are gradually yellowing and dying. The plant is otherwise healthy. What are the most likely explanations, and what information would you want before deciding whether this is normal or a problem?

ASSISTANT: This pattern of new growth at the top while older lower leaves yellow and drop is often a sign that your plant is naturally aging or shedding its oldest foliage to make room for new energy, which can be perfectly normal if the rest of the plant looks vigorous. However, it could also indicate issues like underwatering, nutrient deficiencies—particularly nitrogen—or root stress from overwatering or poor drainage. To determine whether this is a cause for concern, I would want to know what kind of plant it is, how you’re watering it and with what frequency, whether the soil stays moist or dries out between waterings, and if the yellowing leaves are spotted or uniformly pale before they fall off.
