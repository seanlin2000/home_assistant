However, comparing advertised rents has significant risks. The data shows asking prices are not signed lease rents; many listings are inflated to filter tenants or reflect concessions that aren't final. Rent stabilized units under Order #58 have a 0% increase for two-year leases starting October 1, 2026, which means some landlords may advertise higher rates but be legally restricted from increases once the lease is signed. Without knowing your unit's rent stabilization status, using advertised numbers creates misleading comparisons that can make your offer seem uncompetitive when it may actually be below market for a stabilized unit.

If you're offering 4% on an unsubsidized market rate unit, you're likely underbidding; if it's a rent-stabilized unit, the increase is already capped at zero and you'd be overpaying relative to what the law allows.
