We designed and implemented KVMem for Qwen3.8-27B: a new way for llama.cpp to manage conversation memory. The basic idea is simple. A 16GB card cannot keep a full 256K KV cache in VRAM, so we do not try. Finished history lives in regular RAM; for each new question the GPU only loads a limited window of the pieces that matter. We checked this at 256K on LongMemEval-S and AgentLongBench: a 32K GPU window is essentially as accurate as keeping the full 256K history (85.6% vs 86.6% accuracy, 60.9% vs 59.5% task success).

ASSISTANT: The short answer from the sources is yes, but only in reduced form. Qwen3.8's local model is the 27 billion parameter version, and at standard 4-bit it needs more than 16 gigabytes, so on a 4060 Ti you'd run a 3-bit or a trimmed 4-bit build with limited context. Estimates put it around 15 to 25 tokens a second, and one estimator says it drops to just 2 to 4 tokens a second if you force the full 4-bit version and spill into system RAM. That isn't enough to predict a good voice experience. Voice depends on how fast the first word arrives, and that's dominated by processing the prompt, tool results, and search pages, not the generation speed that benchmarks report. It also depends on turning off Qwen's default thinking mode, which can burn thousands of tokens before answering. Then there's how reliably it calls tools at that compression, whether speech recognition and text-to-speech also fit in the same memory, and how long your context gets. You'd need to test with a real prompt and measure time to first spoken word.
