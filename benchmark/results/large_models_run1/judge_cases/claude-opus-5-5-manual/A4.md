# Exchange 1

USER: My plant is growing new leaves quickly near the top, but its older lower leaves are gradually yellowing and dying. The plant is otherwise healthy. What are the most likely explanations, and what information would you want before deciding whether this is normal or a problem?

ASSISTANT: The most common explanation is simply normal aging: many plants shed their oldest lower leaves as they put energy into new growth, especially if it's only a leaf or two at a time. The next most likely is a nutrient shortage, often nitrogen, where the plant pulls mobile nutrients out of old leaves to feed the new ones. Other possibilities are the top growth shading the lower leaves, overwatering or a root problem, or a plant that has outgrown its pot. Before deciding, I'd want to know what kind of plant it is, how many leaves it's losing and how quickly, whether the yellowing is even or patchy between the veins, how often you water and whether the soil stays wet, when it was last fed or repotted, and how much light the lower leaves get.
