# Exchange 1

USER: My projector is connected to a JBL Bluetooth speaker. Sometimes the audio becomes choppy when I connect my laptop, and playing video games on the projector causes more eye strain than playing on my monitor. What could cause these problems, and how would you systematically troubleshoot them?

ASSISTANT: I’ll cover both issues separately, since they have different root causes.

**Choppy Bluetooth audio when a laptop connects**

The most likely culprit is that the projector itself is acting as the Bluetooth source, so your laptop isn’t talking directly to the JBL speaker; it’s sending audio to the projector over HDMI or Wi-Fi, and the projector relays it out to the speaker. That double-hop is where stutter usually lives. Projectors often have weak Bluetooth implementations, and they can fall back from a high-quality codec like aptX or LDAC to SBC when signal conditions get crowded, which sounds choppy at exactly the moments interference spikes.

There are three specific things I’d suspect:

First, bandwidth and interference on the projector’s side. If your laptop is also using Wi‑Fi heavily for gaming or streaming while the projector handles Bluetooth on 2.4 GHz, you’re sharing the same crowded band. Many projectors have poor shielding and small antennas.

Second, distance and line of sight. Projectors are often mounted high or behind furniture, putting a wall or shelf between them and the speaker on your desk. Bluetooth at ten feet through drywall is very different from three feet in open air.

Third, the laptop’s own audio path. Some laptops will try to route audio directly to the JBL when it’s paired to both devices, creating a conflict where two sources fight for one speaker. That produces dropouts exactly when you connect or disconnect.

How I’d troubleshoot it systematically:

Start by isolating the chain. Pair the JBL directly to the laptop and play something locally, with the projector off entirely. If audio is clean there, the problem is the projector’s relay. If it still stutters, the problem is between the laptop and the speaker.

Next, move the speaker closer to the projector and remove obstacles. Test at two feet versus eight feet. A big difference tells you it’s signal strength, not software.

Then change the codec or connection mode if your projector exposes Bluetooth settings; some allow forcing a specific profile or disabling high-bitrate modes. On Windows, check the laptop’s device manager for the JBL and make sure it isn’t set to Hands-Free AG Audio, which is a low-quality telephony profile that causes stutter and distortion.

Finally, test whether Wi‑Fi load matters. Turn off the projector’s wireless networking or connect the laptop via Ethernet, then see if the audio steadies. If it does, you’ve found interference between your Wi‑Fi traffic and the projector’s Bluetooth radio.

If none of that fixes it, the practical fix is to bypass the projector entirely: use a Bluetooth transmitter on the projector’s optical or 3.5 mm audio out, which gives you a dedicated, modern radio instead of whatever built-in module the projector has.
