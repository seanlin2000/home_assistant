The ERS Food Price Outlook (FPO) provides data on food prices and forecasts annual food price changes up to 18 months in the future. On a monthly basis, ERS forecasts the annual percentage change in food prices for the current year and, beginning in July each year, for the following year. Forecasts are based on the most recent Bureau of Labor Statistics (BLS) Consumer Price Index (CPI) and Producer Price Index (PPI) data available at the time of release.
The all-items CPI measures the average change over time in prices paid by urban consumers for a representative market basket of consumer goods and services. The CPI for food is the component of the all-items CPI measuring the changes in retail food prices. ERS examines changes in the CPI for food and constructs forecasts of the CPI for food. Changes in the structures of food and agricultural economies add complexity to forecasting the CPI for food. However, these forecasts provide important signals to farmers, processors, wholesalers, consumers, and policymakers.
ERS also analyzes and forecasts the PPI for farm-level agricultural and wholesale food markets. Like the CPI, the PPI measures price changes over time; however, instead of changes in retail prices, the PPI measures the average change in prices paid to domestic producers for their output. The PPI represents data for nearly every industry in the goods-producing sector of the economy. Changes in farm-level and wholesale-level food PPIs are of particular interest in forecasting food CPIs.
ERS typically releases its monthly CPI and PPI forecast updates on the 25th of the month; however, if the 25th falls on a weekend or a holiday, the monthly update will be published earlier. In addition to these monthly updates, ERS also maintains a record of its previous forecasts. Previous CPI and PPI forecasts are available in the “Historical Data” file.

Picking healthy snacks at the grocery store can feel pretty daunting at times. With so many high-protein options on the shelves, it can be hard to know what actually stands out nutritionally. Two staples—plain Greek yogurt and cottage cheese—often end up side by side in that decision.
## Why Greek Yogurt Is a Good Snack
### Rich in Protein
Greek yogurt is best known for its protein content, which helps support satiety and keeps you feeling fuller between meals. Compared to traditional yogurt, it's strained to remove more of the whey, resulting in a thicker texture and a more concentrated source of protein.
## Why Cottage Cheese Is a Good Snack
### Rich in Protein
Both Greek yogurt and cottage cheese are excellent sources of protein, making them ideal choices for a high-protein snack. Greek yogurt provides 23 g of protein per cup, while cottage cheese edges it out slightly with 24 g per cup.
## Greek Yogurt vs. Cottage Cheese: Which Is Better?
From a protein standpoint, they're nearly identical—Greek yogurt provides about 23 grams per cup, while cottage cheese offers around 24 grams. In other words, you're getting a substantial, filling dose of protein either way.
Where you may see more of a difference is in sodium. Greek yogurt is naturally much lower in sodium, with around 80 milligrams per cup, while cottage cheese can contain 700 milligrams or more, depending on the brand. If you're keeping an eye on sodium intake, that's something to consider—though lower-sodium cottage cheese options are available.
Both also support bone health, thanks to nutrients like calcium and magnesium. Greek yogurt has a slight edge here, providing a bit more of both per serving, but cottage cheese still contributes meaningful amounts. Regularly including either one can help you meet your daily needs for these bone-supporting nutrients.
## How to Enjoy Both as High-Protein Snacks
Both Greek yogurt and cottage cheese are easy to build into balanced, satisfying snacks. Because they're rich in protein, pairing them with fiber-rich carbohydrates and healthy fats can help make them even more filling.

Changes in the structures of food and agricultural economies add complexity to forecasting the CPI for food. However, these forecasts provide important signals to farmers, processors, wholesalers, consumers, and policymakers.
ERS also analyzes and forecasts the PPI for farm-level agricultural and wholesale food markets. Like the CPI, the PPI measures price changes over time; however, instead of changes in retail prices, the PPI measures the average change in prices paid to domestic producers for their output. The PPI represents data for nearly every industry in the goods-producing sector of the economy. Changes in farm-level and wholesale-level food PPIs are of particular interest in forecasting food CPIs.
ERS typically releases its monthly CPI and PPI forecast updates on the 25th of the month; however, if the 25th falls on a weekend or a holiday, the monthly update will be published earlier. In addition to these monthly updates, ERS also maintains a record of its previous forecasts. Previous CPI and PPI forecasts are available in the “Historical Data” file.
