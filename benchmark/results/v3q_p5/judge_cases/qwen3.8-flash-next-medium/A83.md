ASSISTANT: Your twenty thousand dollars buys 0.0333 percent of the company at a sixty million dollar post-money valuation.

Fifty percent cumulative dilution means later rounds cut that stake in half, down to about 0.0167 percent. Applied to a one billion dollar sale, that stake is worth roughly $166,667 before taxes.

That's an 8.33x return on your money, for a profit of about $146,667.

Two things worth flagging, since this is a rough model rather than a term sheet. First, I read "50 percent cumulative dilution" as your percentage ownership being halved overall; if instead you meant the company issues 50 percent more shares in total, the dilution is smaller — about a third rather than a half — and your stake would be closer to $222,000. Second, this ignores liquidation preferences, which is the real risk in early startup rounds: investors in the later rounds often get paid out first, and a billion dollar exit on paper can still leave a small common holder with much less than their percentage suggests. Taxes are also excluded, as you asked, but the character of the gain matters a lot here, since a qualifying small business stock holding of more than five years can be largely tax-free.
