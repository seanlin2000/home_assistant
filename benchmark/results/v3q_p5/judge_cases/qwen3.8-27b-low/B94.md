## Consecutive days or Flex?
The standard pass runs on consecutive days: an 8-day pass covers 8 days in a row. The Swiss Travel Pass Flex (from CHF 289) gives you the same number of travel days to use on any dates within one month — you activate each day before your first ride.
The honest rule: if you are touring Switzerland town-to-town, consecutive days is simpler and cheaper. Flex only wins when you will stay put for several days (a week in one ski resort, a work trip with weekend travel) and genuinely won’t ride on the in-between days.
## How many days do you need?
- Trip of 3–5 days: a 3 or 4-day pass matching your travel days — or compare with the Half Fare Card, which can win on short trips with few train rides.
- One-week trip: the 8-day pass is the sweet spot — it costs only CHF 40 more than 6 days and covers arrival and departure days, including your airport trains.
- Two weeks: the 15-day pass at CHF 499 works out under CHF 34 per day — less than a single Zurich–Lucerne return.
## How you actually use the pass (the part nobody explains)
- Flex users: activate before you board. With the Flex pass you activate each travel day before your first ride, and a “day” is a calendar day, not 24 hours.

## How the Half Fare Card works
Unlike the Swiss Travel Pass, the Half Fare Card is not a ticket — it is a discount card. You still buy a ticket for every journey (easiest in the SBB app, where you set Half Fare once and every price shown is already halved), but you pay 50% of the fare, every time, for one month. There is nothing to activate and no travel days to count.
## FAQ – Swiss Half Fare Card
### Is the Half Fare Card a ticket?
No — it is a discount card, not a ticket. You still buy a ticket for every ride, just at half price. If you board without buying a ticket you can be fined, unlike with the Swiss Travel Pass which is itself the ticket.
