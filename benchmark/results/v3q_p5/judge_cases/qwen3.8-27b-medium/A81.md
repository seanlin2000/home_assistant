ASSISTANT: Good question, and it's a place where a lot of "local AI" setups quietly leak more than people expect. Here's how I'd break it down.

What stays local is the model and everything the model does. The weights, your prompts, the conversation history, the reasoning, and the final answer all get processed on your own hardware. None of that touches the search provider. If the model runs through Ollama, LM Studio, or Open WebUI in local mode, the inference is genuinely on your machine. That part of the privacy promise holds.

What gets transmitted is the search step. When the model decides it needs a fact, the search query it composes goes out to the provider over the internet. And that query is often the sensitive part — models tend to write queries like "best treatment for [your condition]" or "who is the CEO of [your employer]," carrying more context than you'd say out loud. Along with the query, the provider sees your API key (which ties every query to your account), your IP address, and a user agent string. Some services like Ollama's own hosted web search, Tavily, Serper, or Brave are explicitly sending the query to their cloud with your key attached — Ollama's docs even note that search queries leave your machine even though inference doesn't. Depending on the provider's policy, those queries may be logged for days or longer for abuse review, and some providers can require you to pay extra for zero-retention mode. If you also use a page-fetch feature, the URLs you request get sent too, and sometimes the fetched page content round-trips through the provider.

So the pattern is: your full prompts stay home, but the distilled version of your question — plus your identity — goes to a third party.

To minimize exposure, the most effective move is to stop sending queries to a vendor at all. You can self-host SearXNG, which is a free, open-source metasearch engine that runs as a single Docker container. Your local model calls its JSON API on your own network, and SearXNG fans the query out to dozens of public engines — so no single company builds a profile of you, and there's no API key or billing account attached to the queries. Tucked behind a VPN, even the engines see the VPN's exit IP rather than yours. The honest trade-off is that public engines sometimes rate-limit it, so it's a little less reliable than a paid API, and you have to keep its port off the internet since its API has no built-in authentication.

If you'd rather keep a commercial API, a few habits help. Have your local model rewrite the query to strip personal details before it goes out — asking it to paraphrase the question generically takes a lot of the sensitivity out of what's transmitted. Choose providers that publish a real retention policy, and look for zero-data-retention options if you work with anything regulated. And check your local stack too: some front-ends have their own telemetry, and if your Ollama or web UI port is reachable from other devices on your network, "local to my machine" and "local to my house" are different threat levels.

One caveat: none of this addresses prompt injection — a malicious web page can still try to manipulate a local model's behavior through the search results it reads. That's a separate problem from data leaking out, but worth keeping in mind for a setup like this.
