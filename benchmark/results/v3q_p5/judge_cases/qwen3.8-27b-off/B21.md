ASSISTANT: That premise doesn't hold up — the Fed didn't cut rates to zero last month. In fact, the opposite is true. At its October 27-28 meeting, the committee actually *raised* the target range by a quarter point to 3.75% to 4%, and then held it there on October 28 with a 10-2 vote. Two members even wanted to hike further. So we're in a tightening cycle, not one that's been wound down to zero — which makes "should I refinance" a much less obvious yes than you might have assumed.

That said, whether refinancing makes sense for *you* depends on your own numbers, which the Fed decision alone can't tell us. A few things worth weighing:

First, what's your current rate versus what 30-year mortgage rates are actually quoting right now? Mortgage rates don't track the fed funds rate one-to-one, and with inflation running hot (PCE around 3.4% as of last month) and markets pricing in a possible December hike, long-term mortgage rates have been elevated rather than depressed. If your existing rate is already low, there may be little to gain.

Second, the math on refinancing itself: you need the monthly savings to outweigh closing costs over the time you'll actually stay in the home. A rule of thumb is that it usually takes two to three years of savings to recoup a few thousand dollars in fees. If you're planning to move or sell within a couple of years, refinancing often doesn't pencil out no matter what the Fed does.

If you tell me your current rate, how much you still owe, and roughly when you'd plan to leave the home, I can run the break-even numbers for you against whatever rate you're being quoted. Otherwise, I'd be guessing at the part that actually matters.
