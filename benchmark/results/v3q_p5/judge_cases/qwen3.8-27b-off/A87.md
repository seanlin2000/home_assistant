USER: My projector is connected to a JBL Bluetooth speaker. Sometimes the audio becomes choppy when I connect my laptop, and playing video games on the projector causes more eye strain than playing on my monitor. What could cause these problems, and how would you systematically troubleshoot them?

ASSISTANT: Good question, and both of those are pretty common. Let me take them one at a time.

**The choppy audio when your laptop connects**

The most likely culprit is Bluetooth bandwidth contention. A Bluetooth speaker has a limited channel, and it usually only supports one active connection at a time (or two in "multipoint" mode, but the second device often takes priority or degrades quality). So when your laptop pairs — even if you're not actively using it for audio — it can interrupt the projector's stream and cause stuttering, dropouts, or latency spikes.

A few other possibilities worth ruling out:

- **Interference.** Wi-Fi routers, USB 3.0 hubs, microwaves, and other 2.4 GHz devices sit in the same band as most Bluetooth. Moving the speaker away from a router or unplugging a nearby USB 3.0 hub can help.
- **Distance and obstacles.** Bluetooth audio degrades noticeably beyond about 10 meters, especially through walls or furniture. If the laptop is on one side of the room and the speaker on the other, that alone can cause choppy playback.
- **Outdated firmware or drivers.** Both the projector's Bluetooth stack and your laptop's Bluetooth adapter benefit from updates. Check for firmware on the projector (usually in its settings menu) and Bluetooth driver updates on the laptop.
- **Audio codec mismatch.** If the projector is negotiating a lower-quality or less stable codec when two devices are connected, that can introduce artifacts. Forcing a specific profile, if your speaker or projector allows it, sometimes helps.

**How I'd troubleshoot it systematically:**

1. Disconnect the laptop entirely and confirm the audio is clean with only the projector connected. If it's still choppy, the problem is not the laptop — look at interference, distance, or firmware instead.
2. If disconnecting fixes it, reconnect the laptop but turn off its Bluetooth (or unpair it from the speaker). If that resolves the issue, you've confirmed multipoint contention.
3. Try moving the speaker closer to the projector and away from Wi-Fi routers and USB 3.0 devices. Test again.
4. Update firmware on the projector and Bluetooth drivers on the laptop, then retest.
5. If your speaker supports it, disable multipoint so only one device can connect at a time, or manually disconnect the laptop whenever you're using the projector.
6. As a last resort, consider switching to a wired connection (3.5 mm aux or optical) for the projector-to-speaker link, which eliminates Bluetooth entirely and is far more reliable for video.
