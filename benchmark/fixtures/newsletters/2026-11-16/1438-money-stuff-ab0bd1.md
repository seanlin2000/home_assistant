## Hypothetical widgets

Some hypotheticals. What if the board of a widget company met in secret and decided to stop making widgets? Who could stop it? Nobody, really. That is sort of the point of a board.

Of course nobody actually does this. I am being silly. But the reason nobody does it is not the law, exactly; it is that the shareholders would fire the board the next morning.

> Widgetron Inc. said Monday that its board "regularly reviews all strategic options," according to a statement.

I don't know what that means either, but it is the sort of thing a board says.[1]

[1] It probably means nothing. Boards review strategic options the way I review my email: constantly and without enthusiasm.

## Procurement

Anyway, a pretend deputy secretary of widgets steered companies bidding on a government widget contract toward hiring one particular investment bank, according to people familiar with the matter.

The bank's fee would have been about $40 million. The department said the official "never directed anyone to hire anyone."

Suggesting a banker to the other side of a negotiation is unusual. Suggesting the one who used to employ you is more unusual.
